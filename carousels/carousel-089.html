The Hourly Trap
The hourly billing trap —
punishing your own efficiency.
Getting better at your craft makes you earn less. That's broken.

Hourly billing creates a perverse incentive: the more efficient you become, the less you earn. A task that took 3 hours last year takes 45 minutes now — your skills grew, your revenue shrank.

Clients who pay by the hour watch the clock. Clients who pay for outcomes judge by results. One model creates friction. The other creates partnership.

The compounding loss: Every skill improvement under hourly billing costs you revenue. Every hour saved = revenue lost. The better you get, the worse the model treats you.

Determine Value Price
How to determine a
value-based price.
Price at 20-30% of the value you create.

Step 1: Understand the problem in financial terms ('if this generates 2 new clients, what is that worth?'). Step 2: Estimate the value your work creates (not the time — the outcome). Step 3: Price at 20-30% of that value.

If your work generates £50,000 in client revenue, a £10,000-£15,000 engagement is an excellent ROI for them and excellent revenue for you. The math is the conversation.

The Outcome Conversation
The outcome conversation —
finding the value number.
Price after the value anchor, never before.

Before any proposal, ask:

'What does solving this problem mean financially for your business?'
'What's the cost of not solving it in the next 12 months?'
'If we achieve [goal] in 90 days, what would you consider a fair investment?'

These questions establish the value anchor before you mention a number. Price after, not before. The prospect sets the frame. You set the price within it.

The sequence: Understand value → anchor on outcome → price at a fraction of value. In that order. Every time.

Others Charge Less
Handling the 'others charge
less' objection.
The confidence to walk away makes the premium believable.

'I'm sure they do. The question is: are you optimising for price or for outcome?'

Never justify your price — explain the value. Never discount — reframe. 'My clients pay more and achieve more. That's the trade. If the outcome I described isn't worth the investment to you, we may not be the right fit — and that's okay.'

The confidence to walk away is what makes the premium believable.

The Transition
Moving from hourly to value pricing
without losing clients.
Most service businesses 3-5x revenue within 12 months.

Start with new clients only. Don't retroactively change existing clients. For each new engagement: calculate the outcome value first, then price.

Track your effective hourly rate in both models — the comparison is persuasive. Most service businesses 3-5x their revenue within 12 months of this transition.

The first value-priced engagement feels uncomfortable. The second feels normal. The third feels like the old model was the strange one.

The proof point: Calculate your effective hourly rate on your last 3 value-priced projects. Compare to your stated hourly rate. The gap tells the whole story.
