Keyword Research
Keyword research for YouTube —
finding what your clients actually search.
Real searches come from real people with real problems.

The YouTube autocomplete method: go to YouTube, type your topic, and note the suggestions that appear. These are real searches by real people. Start with "how to [your service]" and "best [your service] for [your client type]."
Free tools: TubeBuddy and vidIQ (both have free tiers) show you search volume and competition. The sweet spot: moderate search volume with low competition. This is where small channels can rank fast and attract exactly the right viewer.

Title Formula
Writing titles that rank
and get clicked.
A title must do two jobs: rank in search and earn the click.

Include the exact keyword in the first 40 characters — this is what YouTube reads for ranking. Add a number or power word to increase click-through rate. The title must make a specific promise.
The test: would your ideal client click this title if it appeared in a search result? If not, rewrite it. Most creators write titles they find clever. Your viewer doesn't care about clever — they care about "is this the answer to my specific problem?" Your title must say yes, clearly.

Technical SEO
Descriptions, tags, and chapters —
the technical SEO checklist.
Small technical details compound into significant ranking advantages.

The first 150 characters of your description appear in search results — make them count. Include your primary keyword in the first sentence. Add timestamps and chapters: they create rich snippets that appear in Google search and give viewers a reason to click.
Tags: use 5–10 specific tags, not 50 generic ones. Specificity beats volume. A tag like "content strategy for consultants" outperforms "marketing" every time because it tells YouTube exactly who this video is for.

Thumbnails
Thumbnails — the click-through
rate multiplier.
Custom thumbnails get 2–3x more clicks than auto-generated.

Face + text + high contrast = the thumbnail formula that works across every niche. Test 2 thumbnails per video in the first month using YouTube Studio's A/B feature.
CTR below 4% = thumbnail problem. Retention below 50% = content problem. These two metrics together tell you exactly what to fix. A great thumbnail gets the click. A great video earns the watch time. You need both — but fix your thumbnail first because without the click, the content never gets seen.

Playlists
The playlist strategy that keeps
viewers watching longer.
Playlists get indexed separately — they're free SEO real estate.

Playlists are indexed separately in YouTube search — a playlist about "content strategy for consultants" can rank alongside individual videos on the same topic. Group videos by topic or viewer journey. Put your strongest video first — first impressions apply to playlists too.
YouTube rewards channels that keep people on the platform. Playlists are the single best mechanism for achieving this. A viewer who watches 3 videos in a playlist is 5x more likely to subscribe than a viewer who watches 1 standalone video.
