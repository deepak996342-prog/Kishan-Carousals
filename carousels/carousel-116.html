Why It Compounds
Why Speaking
Compounds So Fast
One appearance creates assets that work for months after the event.

Speaking accelerates brand-building in ways that posting alone cannot match. Four compounding mechanisms:

Borrowed credibility. When a respected event or show hosts you, their credibility transfers to you instantly. The audience trusts you before you've said a word — because someone they already trust invited you.

Captive audience who chose to attend. A conference or podcast listener has self-selected for your topic. They're not scrolling past your content. They've opted in to 30–60 minutes of concentrated trust-building.

Video asset. Every speaking appearance creates a recording. That 45-minute session becomes 90 days of clips, quotes, and repurposed content.

Warm self-selected leads. People who attend specifically because of your topic are pre-qualified. They already want what you offer.

Your First Opportunity
How to Get Your
First Speaking Slot
Start accessible. Build the record. Doors open from evidence.

You don't need to wait for an invitation to a major conference. Start with the venues where the barrier to entry is lowest and work upward.

Podcasts are the easiest entry point. A 3-sentence pitch to a niche podcast host is your first speaking engagement. No travel, no logistics, immediate reach.

Virtual summits are actively looking for speakers with niche expertise. Pitch 3 specific talk ideas with clear audience benefit.

Local industry meetups — in-person communities in your city that gather around your area of expertise.

LinkedIn Audio Events — host your own from your profile today. Build your own audience before asking to borrow someone else's.

The Signature Talk
Creating a Signature
Talk That Converts
One core idea. One memorable framework. One clear audience action.

A signature talk is not a presentation of everything you know. It's a single, carefully constructed argument that moves an audience from where they are to where they need to be — and leaves them knowing exactly what to do next.

One core idea. What's the single most important thing this audience needs to understand? Build everything else around that.

One memorable named framework. Give your methodology a name. The audience should be able to remember it when they get home.

One specific audience action. What do you want them to do in the next 24 hours? Make it concrete, achievable, and directly connected to the core idea.

Clear CTA ending. Every talk ends with an invitation. DM you. Join your newsletter. Book a call. Never leave without a next step.

Getting Booked
The System for
Getting Booked
Speaking opportunities don't come to those who wait — they come to those who pitch.

Speaker one-sheet. One page: your photo, your bio, your talk topics with descriptions, your social proof (past stages, audience sizes), and your contact. Update it every 90 days.

3-minute video reel. A compilation of your best speaking moments — energy, clarity, audience response. This is what every event organiser asks for before booking you.

Proactive pitching: 5 per week. Cold pitch 5 podcasts, summits, or events every week. Keep a simple tracking spreadsheet. Most will say no. That's expected. You only need one yes per week to fill a speaking calendar.

Referrals from previous hosts. After every appearance, ask: "Who else do you think would benefit from having this conversation?" The best bookings come from warm intros.

Maximising Each Appearance
Turn One Appearance
Into 90 Days of Content
One hour of speaking, extracted properly, feeds months of posts.

Most speakers do the hard work — prepare, travel, deliver — and then leave 90% of the value on the table. Don't.

60-second clips. Extract 3–5 of your most quotable moments. These become standalone social content.

Quote card. Pull your single best line. Design a clean visual. Share it as an image post — highest save rate of any format.

Follow-up insight post. The day after your talk, post: "I just spoke about X. Here's the point the audience responded to most — and why it matters."

Email to list. Share the recording with your email subscribers. Gives them a reason to stay subscribed.

Tag host, share widely. Both parties benefit from amplification. Tag the host and the event — they'll reshare to their audience.
