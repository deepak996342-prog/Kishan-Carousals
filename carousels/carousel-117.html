Why Podcasting Wins
Why Podcast Guesting
Beats Most Channels
No other format gives you 45 minutes to build trust with a warm audience.

Consider what a podcast appearance actually gives you compared to every other content format:

45+ minutes of trust-building. A LinkedIn post gets 2 minutes of attention on a good day. A podcast episode gets 45 minutes of focused listening — often while driving, exercising, or commuting. The listener is alone with your voice, your thinking, and your personality.

Borrowed audience. The host has spent months or years building trust with their audience. When they introduce you, a portion of that trust transfers immediately. You're not starting from zero.

Evergreen content. A blog post from 2021 feels dated. A podcast episode from 2021 can still be discovered and listened to today. The shelf life of podcast content vastly exceeds any social post.

Repurpose forever. One episode becomes dozens of content assets.

Finding Shows
Finding the Right Shows
to Pitch
Niche beats size. A 500-listener niche show beats a 100k generalist every time.

The instinct is to chase the biggest shows. That's usually wrong. A 500-listener show where 100% of the audience is your exact ideal client is more valuable than a 100,000-listener show where 0.1% are relevant.

How to find the right shows:

Search by your client's keywords on Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and Google. Find the shows your ideal client actually listens to.

Check engagement, not just downloads. A show with active listener reviews, community engagement, or LinkedIn shares is more alive than a high-download show with no conversation around it.

Look for shows that have recently featured guests at your level. If they've hosted someone with your background before, they understand your value and are likely open to booking you.

The Perfect Pitch
The 3-Sentence Pitch
That Actually Works
Hosts get dozens of pitches. Yours needs to answer three questions instantly.

A podcast pitch email has one job: make the host immediately understand why their audience will benefit from having you on. Three sentences. Three questions answered.

Sentence 1: Who you are + one credibility signal. Not your full bio. One specific, relevant detail that establishes why you're worth 45 minutes of their audience's attention.

Sentence 2: Why their audience benefits + what they will learn. Specific, not generic. "Your listeners will walk away with X" beats "I think your audience would find this interesting."

Sentence 3: Three specific episode angles. Give them three talk ideas, ready to go. Remove the friction of figuring out what the episode would be about. The easier you make it to say yes, the more yeses you get.

Template: "I help [niche] do [outcome]. I think your [audience] would benefit from [specific value]. Here are 3 episode angles: [A], [B], [C]."

Crushing the Interview
How to Crush
the Interview
Great guests prepare assets. They don't wing it.

Most guests show up hoping to have a good conversation. The best guests arrive with prepared assets that make the conversation great regardless of what the host asks.

5 go-to stories ready. Stories that illustrate your core points. Specific, real, with named outcomes. Stories are more memorable than arguments.

1 memorable named framework. The thing listeners will remember and repeat when the episode ends. Name it, explain it in under 2 minutes, make it memorable.

3 specific results to quote. Concrete numbers and outcomes from your work. Specificity builds credibility faster than any other signal.

1 clear listener next step. What should a listener do in the next 24 hours? Make it concrete, free, and directly related to the episode topic.

Post-Episode
Post-Episode
Amplification System
The work after the episode is where most of the value gets created.

60-second clip. Your single best moment from the episode. Short, punchy, and ends with a hook that makes people want more. Post as a video on LinkedIn.

Quote card. Your most quotable line, designed as a clean visual. The highest-save-rate format on any platform.

Insight follow-up post. Write a LinkedIn post expanding on the idea that generated the most response in the episode.

Email to your list. Share the full episode with a personal note about what you most enjoyed discussing.

Tag host and share with your network. The host will reshare — doubling the reach of the episode and beginning a relationship that may lead to referrals to other shows.
