The Packaging Problem
Why undefined services are
hard to sell.
Packaged services sell 3x more easily than undefined ones.

'I do content strategy and charge by the hour' is not a product. Prospects struggle to understand the deliverable, timeline, and outcome.

Packaging converts an undefined engagement into a clear product: specific deliverables, timeline, outcome, investment. Packaged services sell 3x more easily because buyers can evaluate them without needing a discovery call first.

The easier it is to understand what they'll get, the easier it is to say yes.

The test: Can a cold prospect read your service description and understand exactly what they'll have at the end, by when, and for how much? If not, it needs packaging.

Outcome-Based Package
The outcome-based package
structure that converts.
Specific, time-bounded, outcome-described packages convert browsers to buyers.

Instead of 'content strategy services': 'LinkedIn Authority Package — 90 days. You'll have an optimised profile, 30 pieces of published content, 200 new strategic connections, and a documented content system you own. Investment: £3,500.'

Specific. Time-bounded. Outcome-described. The vague description sends prospects to competitors. The specific package converts browsers to buyers.

3-Package Architecture
The 3-package good–better–best
architecture.
The middle tier is your real product.

Starter: Limited scope, low-risk entry, intro price. Core: Your primary service, most popular, best value (60–70% of sales). Premium: Comprehensive, full access, maximum outcome.

The middle tier is your real product — anchor the premium high to make core look reasonable. The starter creates access for cautious buyers who often upgrade.

Three prices. One architecture. Maximum conversion.

The anchor effect: A £8,000 premium makes the £4,000 core feel like a bargain. Price the premium first — let it do the work of making your actual offer look reasonable.

Package Names
Package names that sell —
why labels matter.
Names that reflect transformation sell better than tier labels.

'Bronze, Silver, Gold' = commodity. 'LinkedIn Launchpad → LinkedIn Authority → LinkedIn Dominance' = a journey of transformation.

Names that reflect outcomes and progression sell better than tier labels. Test with 5 ideal clients before committing.

The right name creates instant clarity about what the outcome is and who it's for. Your package name is the first impression of the value — make it do work.

Bonuses
Bonuses that convert
hesitant buyers.
Bonuses are pre-emptive objection-handling.

Identify your clients' 3 most common hesitations:

'What if I get stuck implementing this?' → add an implementation call bonus. 'What if my situation changes?' → add a pause option. 'What if results take longer than expected?' → add a 30-day extension.

Each bonus removes a barrier to saying yes. Bonuses don't add cost — they remove resistance. The best bonus is one that directly answers your most common objection.

The bonus principle: Every bonus you include should answer a specific 'what if' your ideal client is silently asking before they buy.
