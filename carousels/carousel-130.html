Why 60 Minutes Is Enough
Most content time is wasted
on decisions, not writing.
A system eliminates the decision phase entirely.

If you've ever spent 90 minutes "working on content" and published nothing, the problem wasn't time. It was decision fatigue.

What to write about. Which angle to take. What format to use. When to post. These micro-decisions, multiplied across 4 posts, consume hours. The system pre-answers all of them.

Once you have a content bank, a format library, and a publishing schedule, writing is the only variable. And writing 4 posts takes 28 minutes when you're not simultaneously trying to invent them.

Time audit: Track your next content session by phase. Most people spend 70% on deciding, 30% on writing. The fix: decide in batch on Sunday.

Sunday — Week Prep (10 min)
Make all decisions before
the week begins.
10 minutes of planning saves 60 minutes of friction.

Open your content bank. Pull 4 topics — one per post day. Assign a format to each: text post, image post, carousel, or story format. Set the posting days and times (block them in your calendar). Done.

You've just answered every question you'd otherwise have to answer while staring at a blank screen on Monday morning. The week is planned. Monday writing is pure execution — no cognitive overhead, no deliberation, no procrastination.

This 10-minute investment is the highest ROI in the system.

Monday — 30-Min Write
4 posts. 7 minutes each.
No editing allowed.
Speed is the creative constraint that forces clarity.

Set a timer for 7 minutes. Write post 1. Stop. Set timer again. Write post 2. Four posts in 28 minutes, leaving 2 minutes buffer.

The template: Hook → 3 insight bullets → CTA. This constraint keeps you out of your own head. No editing during writing — if you write a bad sentence, leave it and keep going. Bad sentences get cut on Tuesday, not Monday.

Writing and editing are different cognitive modes. Mixing them creates the worst of both: slow writing and weak editing. Separate the modes.

The 7-minute rule: If you can't explain an idea clearly in 7 minutes of writing, you don't understand it well enough yet. That's also useful data.

Tuesday — 20-Min Polish
Edit with discipline.
Schedule with confidence.
5 minutes per post. One job: remove friction.

5 minutes per post: read it aloud (catches awkward phrasing instantly), cut the first sentence of 3 out of 4 posts (the real opener is usually sentence 2), sharpen one CTA so it's specific not generic.

Then schedule all 4 posts. Buffer, Hootsuite, LinkedIn's native scheduler — any works. The act of scheduling removes the daily decision of "should I post today?" That decision doesn't happen during the week. It happened on Sunday.

The week is done. Four posts, 60 minutes total.

The Compound Effect
Week 1 is hard.
Week 12 is automatic.
Systems improve with repetition. Motivation doesn't.

Week 1: the system feels clunky. You'll go over time. You'll doubt the quality. That's fine — your brain is learning a new pattern. The friction is temporary.

Week 4: the rituals are forming. Sunday planning takes 8 minutes. Monday writing flows. Tuesday polishing is mechanical.

Week 12: the system runs itself. You stop watching the clock because you're inside the habit. The content bank gets faster to navigate. The format templates get faster to execute. Each week's output improves while time spent decreases.

Do not miss a week. Missing week 1 means starting over. The system only works if you run it, imperfectly, until it's automatic. The first 4 weeks are the hardest.
