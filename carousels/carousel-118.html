Why Pursue It
Why Media Coverage
Is Worth Pursuing
One feature creates credibility assets you use across everything forever.

Media coverage does three things that content alone cannot:

Borrowed credibility at scale. When Forbes, Fast Company, or even a well-regarded industry publication features you, their editorial authority transfers. Your ideal clients see it as a third-party validation — someone else's team decided you were worth featuring. That's qualitatively different from you writing about yourself.

Permanent SEO backlinks. A mention in an established publication creates a backlink that improves your website's search ranking for years. Unlike social content, it compounds silently in the background without any ongoing effort from you.

"As featured in" proof you use forever. Once published, those logos belong on your website, LinkedIn profile, and sales materials — permanently. They don't expire.

HARO Strategy
HARO: The Easiest
First Media Win
Set up alerts. Respond fast. Be specific. Get quoted.

Help a Reporter Out (HARO) sends journalist queries to your inbox daily. Journalists are literally asking for expert sources — and most people who sign up never respond. That's your opportunity.

Set up alerts for your exact topic areas. You want to be notified the moment a relevant query arrives.

Respond within 1–2 hours. Journalists work on tight deadlines. Late responses almost never get used, regardless of quality. Speed matters as much as substance.

Give a direct, quotable answer. Don't send a pitch. Answer the question they asked, clearly and specifically, in 2–4 sentences. Then add one sentence of credential. Generic, vague answers get ignored. The more specific your answer, the more likely it is to be used verbatim.

Cold Pitching
Cold Pitching
Publications That Work
Find the editor. Lead with value. Write their opening sentence for them.

Cold pitching media works when you approach it as a service, not a promotion. The editor isn't looking for a way to help you — they're looking for content that serves their readers.

Find the editor's email. Hunter.io, LinkedIn, or the publication's masthead. Not the generic contact form — that's where pitches go to die.

Lead with data or a contrarian angle. "Here's a counterintuitive finding from my 6 months of testing LinkedIn content formats" is more compelling than "I'd love to write for you."

Write the opening sentence of the article for them. Show them exactly what the article would say. Editors who can see the piece in their head are far more likely to say yes.

The pitch email should be under 150 words. Long pitches signal that you don't respect their time.

Journalist Relationships
Building Journalist
Relationships That Last
Be the expert they call first — before they ever need to search for you.

The best media coverage doesn't come from pitches — it comes from being a reliable, accessible expert that journalists already know and trust.

Follow journalists covering your space on LinkedIn. Not just to watch their work — to understand their angle, their interests, and the stories they're currently working on.

Comment with genuine insight. Not "great article!" — but an actual added perspective, a data point, or a relevant disagreement. Over time, your name becomes familiar. Familiar names get called when deadlines arrive.

Be their go-to expert. When a journalist needs a quote quickly, they search their recent interactions first, not Google. Position yourself in those interactions by being consistently helpful, specific, and available. That's the relationship that produces consistent coverage.

Byline Articles
Byline Articles:
Your Own Words, Their Platform
A byline turns your expertise into permanent published authority.

A byline article is a piece you write that gets published under your name in a third-party publication. It carries all the authority of the platform and keeps your voice entirely.

Pitch 600-word opinion pieces with your byline to industry publications, LinkedIn Pulse, and niche blogs. The bar for byline content is lower than you think — editors need a constant supply of expert perspectives and rarely have enough contributors.

The best byline pitches lead with a strong opinion on a contested issue in your field. Not "5 tips for better content" — but "Why the engagement-first content strategy is destroying solopreneur businesses."

Industry blogs, LinkedIn Pulse, and niche publications all count. Start with the accessible ones. Build the publication list. Each byline becomes a logo on your website and a signal to every prospective client who Googles you.

Media Coverage Playbook — 4 Channels with Timelines & Tips

Answer in under 150 words, highly specific

Cold Pitch (Month 1)
Build a list of 20 target publications
Find the right editor for each via LinkedIn
Send 5 pitches per week, personalised
Lead with data or contrarian angle
Follow up once after 5 business days
