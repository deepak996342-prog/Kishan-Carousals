Why Leads Go Cold
Why leads go cold — it's
almost never about you.
Budget cycles, shifting priorities, and timing windows.

Most cold leads aren't cold because of you. Budget cycles change. Priorities shift. Timing moves. The prospect who ghosted in January may be ready in April. Your job: stay present and relevant so that when they're ready, you're first.

The 44% of salespeople who quit after one follow-up are leaving money on the table. The business doesn't go away — it goes to whoever stayed patient and persistent.

The mindset shift: A cold lead is not a lost lead. It's a lead waiting for the right timing. Your follow-up sequence keeps you visible until that moment arrives.

The 5-Touch Sequence
What to say and when —
the complete 5-touch system.
Each touch has a purpose. None of them pitch.

Touch 1 (Day 2): Confirm receipt — 'Just checking this reached you.' Touch 2 (Day 7): Share relevant content — no pitch. Touch 3 (Day 14): Brief genuine check-in. Touch 4 (Day 30): Case study from a similar client. Touch 5 (Day 60): Graceful close — 'closing out my notes on this.'

After 5 touches with no response: quarterly stay-warm email only. Don't become noise — become a trusted presence.

Value Follow-Ups
The value follow-up that stays
relevant without pitching.
Every follow-up that delivers value feels completely different.

Every follow-up that delivers value feels completely different from one that pitches. 'Thought of you when I saw this.' 'Here's a quick tip for your specific situation.' 'I wrote something this week that directly relates to what you mentioned.'

Value first. Ask later — or let them ask when they're ready. The best follow-ups don't feel like follow-ups. They feel like a trusted contact sharing something genuinely useful.

The rule: Every follow-up should be something the prospect would thank you for — independent of whether they ever buy from you.

The Short Email
The short email structure
that gets replies.
Short emails get replied to. Long follow-ups get ignored.

Subject: '[First name] — quick thought' (6 words, personal). Open: Reference one specific detail from your last conversation. Body: One paragraph — either a value point or a genuine question. Close: One gentle CTA ('Worth a 15-minute call?').

The email that gets the most replies is always the shortest. Respect their time and they'll respect yours with a response.

The Professional Close
When to stop — the close
that often opens things back up.
After 5 touches: one final email.

After 5 touches: one final email. 'I'm going to stop reaching out after this — I don't want to be a nuisance. If the timing ever changes and you want to revisit [the problem], I'd love to connect again.'

This email gets more replies than any other in the sequence. It removes all pressure and often unblocks the conversation. Prospects who were avoiding respond because the pressure is gone and the respect is obvious.

Why it works: Removing pressure creates safety. Safety creates honesty. Honesty either reopens the door or closes it cleanly. Either outcome is better than limbo.
