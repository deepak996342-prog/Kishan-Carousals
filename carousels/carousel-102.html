Why LinkedIn Live Gets

The algorithm actively favours live content.

LinkedIn Live is not just another content format — it's an algorithm shortcut. When you go live, LinkedIn sends real-time notifications to your followers, something no other format receives. The algorithm boosts your broadcast during streaming, prioritising it in feeds across your first and second-degree network.

After the broadcast ends, the replay video stays in your feed as a permanent post — continuing to generate views, reactions, and comments for weeks. One broadcast can outperform a month of regular posts.

Format Choices
3 First-Broadcast
Formats That Work
Pick one and run with it — don't overthink the format.

1. Q&A "Ask Me Anything" — the lowest-prep format. Announce your topic, let your audience ask questions live. Works brilliantly if you have even a modest engaged following. 20–30 minutes is ideal.

2. Topic Deep-Dive — a structured 25-minute commentary on a subject you know deeply. Have 5 talking points ready; let the conversation flow naturally from there.

3. Behind-the-Scenes Process Walkthrough — share your screen, show your tools, walk through exactly how you do something. High perceived value, low production barrier.

Promotion Timeline
The 5-Day Promotion
Sequence Before You Go Live
The broadcast starts long before you press go live.

Day −5: Announce the broadcast with a compelling hook post. State the specific outcome attendees will get. Invite your ideal audience explicitly.

Day −1: Send a reminder post. Add urgency — "Tomorrow at 11am." Create a LinkedIn Event so people can register and receive automatic reminders.

Morning of: Post with the direct broadcast link. Keep it short: what you're covering + the time + the link. Pin this to the top of your profile. Reply to every comment to signal the algorithm this post matters.

Post-Broadcast
How to Repurpose One
Broadcast Into a Week of Content
The broadcast is the source. Repurposing is the strategy.

Most people go live and then… nothing. That's a massive waste. Your broadcast is a content mine. From one 30-minute session you can extract:

60-second highlight clips — pull the 2–3 moments where the energy peaked. Post each one separately over the next week.

A follow-up insight post — share the top 3 things your audience asked about. This second post often outperforms the broadcast itself.

DMs to everyone who commented — they showed up for you. Thank them personally. This is how live sessions become real relationships.
