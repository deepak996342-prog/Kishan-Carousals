Why It Works
Why Origin Stories
Work So Well
Human brains are wired for narrative — not credentials.

Four psychological mechanisms make origin stories the most effective trust-building content you can create:

Narrative processing. We understand and remember stories 22 times better than facts. When you tell a story, the listener's brain fires as if they're experiencing it themselves.

Relatability. Credentials create distance. Struggle creates connection. When you share where you came from, people who've been there recognise you as someone who truly understands.

Trust-shortcutting. A compelling origin story builds in minutes the trust that would normally take months of consistent posting.

Differentiation. Two consultants can have identical credentials but completely different origin stories. The story is what makes you irreplaceable.

The Structure
The 5-Part Origin
Story Structure
Every compelling origin story follows this narrative arc.

1. Normal World. Where were you before? What did your life and work look like? Set the scene so readers can understand the contrast that's coming.

2. Inciting Incident. What happened that broke the Normal World? The event, the failure, the realisation, the conversation that changed everything.

3. The Struggle. What did the transition cost? What did you have to figure out, overcome, or give up? The struggle is where relatability lives.

4. The Transformation. What changed? What did you learn that you didn't know before? What became possible that wasn't before?

5. The Mission. Why do you do this work now? What drives you to help others through what you figured out?

Emotional Truth
The 'Why This Matters'
Element
Beyond skills to the emotional truth that makes your story unforgettable.

Most origin stories list credentials and turning points. The memorable ones go deeper — to the emotional truth underneath the facts.

What happened that made this work meaningful to you? Not just what happened, but how it felt. Not just what you learned, but what it cost you to learn it. Not just where you are now, but why you refuse to go back.

The emotional truth is the element that makes readers recognise themselves in your story. They haven't lived your specific experience, but they've felt the same fear, frustration, or longing. That recognition creates a bond that credentials never can.

Your story doesn't have to be dramatic to be powerful. It has to be honest.

Find your emotional truth: Complete this sentence — "The real reason I do this work is because I was once the person who needed it and couldn't find anyone who truly understood..."

Relevance
Making Your Story
Directly Relevant
Your transformation is your client's roadmap. Connect them explicitly.

The biggest mistake people make with their origin story is telling a compelling personal narrative that doesn't connect to the work they do for clients.

Your transformation is the client's roadmap. You've already been where they are. You've already solved the problem they're now facing. Your story isn't just interesting — it's evidence that the transformation is possible and that you're the person who can guide them through it.

Make this explicit in your story. Don't let the reader do the connection work themselves. Show them directly: "This is where I was. This is what I figured out. This is exactly what I help you do now."

That's not selling. That's service.

Where to Use It
Where to Deploy Your
Origin Story
One story, deployed across every high-leverage touchpoint.

Your origin story is not a one-time post. It's a strategic asset to be deployed every time you encounter a new audience. The same story, adapted for context.

LinkedIn About section. The most-read piece of copy on your entire profile. Your origin story belongs here in full.

Intro post. When you start on a new platform, enter a new community, or want to reset your narrative — lead with your origin.

Podcast appearances. Hosts always ask "how did you get into this?" That's your origin story invitation.

Sales conversations. The first question prospects always ask is "why do you do this?" Your origin is the answer that builds trust before you discuss price.

New audience contexts. Any time 20%+ of your audience is new, reshare your origin story. It's always someone's first time.

Origin Story Builder

Origin Story Template — 5 Sections with Word Count Targets

Normal World + Inciting Incident
Where were you? (50–75 words)
Key question: What was life like before the change?
What happened to break the normal? (50–75 words)
Key question: What was the moment or event that disrupted everything?
Make it specific — a conversation, a failure, a number
The Struggle + Transformation
What was the cost of the transition? (75–100 words)
Key question: What did you have to figure out or overcome?
What changed as a result? (75–100 words)
Key question: What became possible that wasn't before?
Include a specific before/after result or realisation
The Mission
Why do you do this work now? (50–75 words)
Key question: What drives you to help others through this?
Connect explicitly to the client's current situation
End with a clear statement of what you help people achieve
Total target: 300–400 words for a LinkedIn post
Write your first draft in one sitting. Don't edit — just complete each section. The emotional truth is in the unpolished version.
