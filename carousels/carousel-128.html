Monthly Analytics Ritual
30 minutes. Once a month.
Changes everything.
Consistency in review equals improvement in output.

Set a recurring calendar event on the last Friday of every month. Pull three lists: top 5 posts by impressions, top 5 by engagement rate, top 5 by comments.

These three metrics tell you different things. Impressions = algorithm reach. Engagement rate = audience resonance. Comments = emotional response. The sweet spot is posts that score well on all three.

Look for patterns across your lists, not individual post performance. One viral post proves nothing. Repeated high performance reveals your positioning.

Analysing Best Posts
What made your best
posts actually work?
Diagnose before you replicate.

For each top-performing post, ask: What format was it? What topic? What length? What hook style? When was it posted?

Also: Did it include a question? Did it share a personal story? Did it challenge a popular belief? Did it use a numbered list?

Most creators look at total performance. The smart ones look at the variables that drove performance. Format is often less important than topic. Topic is often less important than hook. Hook is always more important than length.

Pattern signal: When 3+ top posts share a common element (same topic, same format, same hook type) — you've found a signal. Exploit it.

Content Intelligence System

3 months of data. 6 months of strategy.

Build a simple spreadsheet. After 3 months you have a dataset. After 6 months you have a strategy. You'll know exactly what to post, when, in what format — because the data tells you, not your intuition.

Adapting Without Abandoning
Follow your data without
losing your voice.
The line between data-informed and data-dictated.

Here's the trap: you notice listicles outperform essays in your analytics. So you post only listicles. Your content becomes formulaic. Engagement drops. You've optimised the format while hollowing out the value.

Don't just copy algorithmic winners. Find the overlap between what your audience wants and what you find worth saying. That intersection is your positioning. Analytics should sharpen your aim — not define your identity.

Your voice is your moat. Data without voice builds a commodity. Voice without data builds slowly. Both together builds authority.

A/B Testing on LinkedIn
Test one variable.
Learn one lesson.
Systematic improvement vs. random hoping.

You can't run true A/B tests on LinkedIn — you can't send different versions to the same audience simultaneously. But you can test sequentially.

Test one variable at a time: same topic, different hook structure. Same hook style, different format. Same format, different posting time. Same topic, personal story vs. data-driven.

The key: change one variable, hold everything else constant, compare results. After 4-6 posts with a controlled variable, you'll have a directional answer.

Most valuable test first: Hook format. Run 4 posts on the same topic with 4 different hook types. Your most valuable content intelligence will come from this.
