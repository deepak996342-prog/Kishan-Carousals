Why Events Work
What LinkedIn Events
Do for Your Brand
Four compounding benefits that make events worth creating.

Warm up cold prospects. Someone who registers for your event self-identifies as interested in your topic. That's intent data you can't buy.

Algorithm boost. LinkedIn promotes events to your connections' networks, extending your reach beyond your current audience.

Email-list alternative. Registered attendees become a list you can communicate with via event updates — inside LinkedIn, no email required.

Establish authority before you say a word. Hosting an event positions you as an expert worth listening to. Attendance builds credibility by association.

Event Design
Creating an Event
People Actually Click On
Your title is your most important marketing decision.

Lead with specific outcome, not format. "How to Generate 3 Qualified Leads/Week with LinkedIn Content" will always outperform "LinkedIn Content Webinar." Tell people exactly what they'll walk away with.

Professional banner matters. A clean, branded banner in the correct 1920×1080px ratio signals that you take this seriously. Use Canva's free LinkedIn Event template.

Clear agenda + strong speaker bio. List exactly what you'll cover in 3–4 bullet points. Your bio should mention one specific result, not just job title. People attend for the outcome, not the credential.

Title formula: "How to [Specific Outcome] in [Time Frame] Using [Your Method]" converts significantly better than generic topic titles.

Promotion Plan
The 30-Day Promotion
Plan That Fills Events
Consistent pre-event content is what separates 20 from 200 attendees.

D-30: Create the event and publish a teaser post. Announce the topic and ask who wants to attend.

D-30 to D-7: Post 3× per week on topics related to your event. Each post ends with a soft invite. This builds anticipation and attracts exactly the right audience.

D-7 to D-1: Send personal invites to your warm connections — the 20–30 people who engage with your content regularly. Personal beats broadcast.

Co-host strategy: Find one person in a complementary space, make them a co-host. Their network sees your event too. Doubles promotion reach overnight.

Running the Event
How to Run a High-Value
LinkedIn Event
Three principles that turn attendees into followers.

Open with a provocative question. "How many of you are posting content on LinkedIn but not seeing any leads from it?" 80% of the room will mentally raise their hand. You've just demonstrated you understand their exact problem.

Involve the chat actively. Ask questions throughout. Call out specific names when reading comments. People who feel seen share what they're attending with their network.

Deliver one breakthrough insight they can use today. Not a list of ten tactics — one clear, actionable idea that makes them think "I need to do this now." That insight is what they'll share and talk about.

Post-Event 72 Hours
The 72-Hour Window
After Every Event
Most of the value from your event happens after it ends.

The 72 hours after your event is your highest-conversion window. Here's the sequence:

Hour 0–6: Post a thank-you post tagging attendees who asked great questions. This gets them to share your post with their network.

Hour 24: Share the recording link via an event update. Everyone who registered gets notified — including those who didn't attend live.

Hour 48–72: Pitch the next step — a free resource, a follow-up conversation, your service. People who attended your event are now pre-sold. They've invested 30–60 minutes with you. This is exactly the right moment to make an offer.
