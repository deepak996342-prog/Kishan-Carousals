Phase 1 · Months 1–3
Find Your Voice.
Consistency before reach.
Don't optimize yet. Post 3×/week and experiment with formats.

The first 90 days are not about followers or reach. They are about discovering what you actually sound like when you write publicly and consistently. Most people have a private voice and a polished professional voice — neither is ideal for LinkedIn. Your real LinkedIn voice is somewhere between them, and you won't find it without publishing.

Post 3 times per week. Try different formats — personal stories, how-to frameworks, opinion takes, carousels, lists. Don't chase the algorithm. Don't optimize hooks yet. Just show up. The posts that resonate will tell you more than any strategy guide. This phase is pure R&D. Treat it that way.

Phase 1 success metric: Not followers or impressions — consistency. Did you post 3×/week for 90 days? Yes = phase 1 complete. No = restart the clock.

Phase 2 · Months 4–6
Find Your Audience.
Follow engagement signals.
1,000 who care is worth more than 10,000 random followers.

By month 4, you have data. You know which content generates comments, saves, and DMs. Now you niche tighter around those signals. Most creators try to appeal to everyone in months 1–3 and wonder why nothing sticks. The phase 2 work is deliberately narrowing.

Follow engagement signals relentlessly: posts that generate DMs = higher niche relevance; posts that generate shares = broader appeal; posts that generate comments with questions = you have an engaged learner audience. Narrow your topic focus to the areas where DMs and questions appear. The narrower you go, the more the right people feel you're speaking directly to them. And those people refer others exactly like themselves.

Niche signal: If a specific type of person consistently engages with a specific topic — that's your niche pairing. Write exclusively to that person on that topic for 90 days.

Phase 3 · Months 7–12
Build Your Reputation.
Own 1–2 specific topics.
Launch your lead magnet or newsletter. Reference your own frameworks.

By month 7 you know your voice, you know your audience, and you know your 2–3 best-performing topic areas. Now it's time to build a reputation as the go-to person for those specific topics. Stop covering everything. Double down on your 2 best topics and become genuinely known for them.

This phase is also when you create your first lead magnet or launch a newsletter — capturing the audience you've built into a channel you own. Start naming and referencing your own frameworks rather than borrowing from others. Every time you say "my XYZ framework" or "the process I use," you're building proprietary intellectual capital. By month 12, your audience should associate your name with 1–2 clear ideas.

Phase 4 · Year 2 H1
Leverage What Works.
Double down. Cut the rest.
Introduce premium offer. Speaking. Podcast guesting.

By year 2, month 1, you have 12 months of data. You know exactly which content series drives enquiries, which formats your audience shares, and what topics position you most effectively. Year 2 H1 is the time to make the ruthless decision: delete everything that doesn't work and invest all energy into what does.

This is also the phase to introduce your premium offer publicly — whether a group program, course, or high-value consulting package. Your audience is warm enough now. Speaking engagements and podcast guesting expand your reach into adjacent audiences while building the kind of credibility that LinkedIn alone cannot provide. Being seen on stage or heard on a podcast changes how people perceive your LinkedIn content retroactively.

Phase 5 · Year 2 H2
Systemize and Scale.
LinkedIn is now a lead engine.
Batch content. Hire help. Build passive income. Remove yourself from the bottleneck.

By the second half of year 2, LinkedIn has become a reliable, predictable lead source. The work now is to systematize what you've built so it doesn't require daily attention to function. Batch your content creation into weekly sessions rather than daily scrambling. Bring in support for distribution, design, or research. Build passive income streams that generate revenue without your daily input.

The goal at the end of 24 months is not to be working harder on LinkedIn than you were in month 1 — it's to be working on LinkedIn in a more strategic, higher-leverage way, while the compounded content library and the audience relationships continue to generate inbound without constant effort. Month 24 LinkedIn is a machine. Month 1 LinkedIn is a habit.
