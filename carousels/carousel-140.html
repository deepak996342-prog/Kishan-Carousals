The 1:1 Ceiling
40–50 billable hours is
your hard limit. Full stop.
Scaling requires a different model, not more hours.

The math of hourly consulting is brutally simple: there are only so many hours in a week. At 40 billable hours and £200/hour, your maximum revenue is £8,000/week — and that assumes 100% utilisation, zero admin, zero marketing, zero illness, zero holidays. The real cap is far lower.

Most consultants hit their revenue ceiling not because they're not good enough, but because they've built a model that can't grow without adding more of their time. The only way to break through the ceiling is to decouple revenue from hours. That requires a fundamentally different service architecture — not working harder, not charging more per hour. A different model entirely.

The ceiling test: If doubling your revenue requires doubling your hours, you have a ceiling. If it requires better systems or more clients per delivery hour, you have a staircase.

The 4 Scalability Levels
Each level multiplies
your revenue per hour.
Progress through levels as proof and systems accumulate.

Level 1: 1:1 custom service. Fully bespoke, high margin, time-capped. Entry point for every consultant.

Level 2: Productized service. Fixed scope, deliverables, timeline, and price. Same work, repeatable process. Doubles capacity without doubling hours.

Level 3: Group delivery. Same preparation, multiple clients. Cohort programs, group coaching, mastermind formats. Revenue per preparation hour multiplies by the group size.

Level 4: Asynchronous / self-service. Courses, digital products, licensing. You deliver the work once; clients consume it on their schedule. Revenue scales without additional delivery time.

Productizing Your Service
Define the scope. Fix the price.
Double your capacity.
A standard engagement alone can transform your business.

Productization doesn't mean selling a commodity — it means creating a repeatable, clearly scoped engagement with defined deliverables, timeline, and price. Instead of "I'll help you with your LinkedIn strategy for £X/hour," it's "12-week LinkedIn Growth System: full profile optimisation, content strategy, 30-day editorial calendar, weekly review calls. £3,000. Fixed."

This alone — without changing your delivery method — transforms your business: proposals become easier to write, expectations become clearer, delivery becomes faster (you've done it before), and clients self-select based on fit rather than negotiating your rates. Productization is the bridge between Level 1 and Level 3.

Productization checklist: Fixed scope · Fixed deliverables · Fixed timeline · Fixed price · Standard intake process · Repeatable delivery template

Group Delivery Models
Same outcome. More clients.
Less time per person.
The economics of group delivery change everything.

Group delivery comes in three primary formats, each with different dynamics:

Group coaching (5–10 clients): Same transformation as 1:1 but delivered in group calls. Clients pay 50–70% of 1:1 rate. Your revenue per hour of delivery can be 3–7× higher.

Cohort program (time-bounded): A structured 6–12 week curriculum with a defined start/end date. Creates community, accountability, and clear milestones. Cohorts allow launch events that generate concentrated revenue.

Mastermind (peer-learning model): You facilitate peer exchange and expertise. Lower preparation overhead. Higher trust required. Premium pricing because the community IS the product.

LinkedIn's Role in the Transition
Build authority that commands
higher prices at lower volume.
Content attracts clients already sold on your approach.

The shift from 1:1 to scalable models is often blocked by one fear: "Will I lose clients if I stop doing custom work?" The answer LinkedIn provides is profound. When your content builds genuine authority, you attract clients who want your specific methodology — not just "a consultant." They're pre-sold before the first conversation.

This allows you to raise prices, narrow your offer, and serve fewer clients at higher value — while simultaneously marketing a group program to the broader audience. Your content educates the mass market. Your 1:1 serves the premium segment. Your group program serves the middle. LinkedIn makes it possible to run all three simultaneously without separate marketing strategies for each.

Transition rule: Don't drop 1:1 until group delivery generates equivalent revenue. Run them in parallel. Let the market tell you which to grow.
