The Core Problem
Why Generalists Get
Outcompeted Every Time
Specificity is not a limitation. It's a competitive moat.

"I help businesses with marketing" competes with 10,000 professionals. "I help B2B SaaS companies reach $1M ARR with content" competes with 20. The math is simple. The execution is not.

Generalists face a brutal reality: they are always competing on price. When your offer sounds like everyone else's, the only remaining differentiator is cost. Clients can't tell why you're different, so they default to whoever is cheapest.

Specialists, by contrast, attract clients who are already sold on the category. The decision isn't "should I hire a content specialist?" — it's "which one?" That's the market position you want.

The Framework
The Niche Selection
Framework
Your niche lives at the intersection of three circles.

The most profitable niche is found where three things converge: what you do exceptionally well, who needs it most, and who pays well for it.

Miss any one of these and the niche breaks down. Skills without paying clients is a hobby. Skills without demand is frustration. Demand without your skills is someone else's business.

Most people get stuck because they focus only on what they're good at. Start instead by asking: who has a painful, specific problem that I can solve better than anyone they could find in 10 minutes of searching? That's your niche entry point.

The 3-circle test: Write down your answer for each circle. Your niche is the phrase that satisfies all three simultaneously — specific, needed, and paid for.

Testing Your Niche
Testing Your Niche
in Public
Don't guess what resonates. Publish and let the market tell you.

The fastest validation method costs nothing and takes two weeks. Create 5 posts that speak directly to your ideal client's most specific problem. Not broad posts. Not general wisdom. Posts that only your target niche would stop scrolling for.

Watch what happens. Who likes? Who comments? Who DMs? Who shares? The engagement pattern is your market signal. If the right people respond, you've found something. If only peers respond, you haven't gone specific enough.

Follow the resonance. The niche you pick in theory is rarely the niche you land on in practice. The market corrects you — if you let it.

The 5-post test: Write 5 posts assuming your ideal client is already found. If those posts generate DMs from strangers, your niche is working.

Sub-Niching
The Riches Are in
the Sub-Niches
Every step toward specificity reduces competition and raises fees.

Consider the progression: Real estate agent → commercial real estate → office leasing → office leasing for tech startups. Each step narrows the audience — and multiplies the premium you can charge.

At each stage, the competition shrinks dramatically. The clients become more specific. The problems become more defined. And critically, the solutions become more valuable because they're not generic — they're built for exactly this person with exactly this problem.

Most people stop niching before they've gone far enough. The fear is always the same: "what if I run out of clients?" The reality is the opposite. Narrow niches have lower competition, higher trust, and clients who actively seek the specialist out.

Niche Evolution
The Niche Evolution
Strategy
Start narrow. Build authority. Expand deliberately.

A niche is not a cage. It's a launchpad. The strategy is: start hyper-specific, build undeniable authority in that narrow space, then expand deliberately once you own it.

The mistake most people make is trying to expand before they've established dominance in the first place. You can't leverage brand authority you haven't yet built. Expand too early and you dilute the very thing that made you valuable.

Give yourself 6–12 months to own a narrow space. When you're the name that comes up in that niche — in referrals, in searches, in conversations — you've built the platform to expand from. Not before.

Expansion rule: Only expand your niche when you're turning away work in the current one. That's the signal you've earned the authority to go broader.

Niche Selection Tool

Niche Selection Matrix — Score Each Factor 1–5

Demand (1–5)
Do enough people have this problem?
Are they actively searching for solutions?
Is there a clear pain point or costly problem?
Are there communities or groups discussing this?
Is demand growing or stable?
Competition (1–5)
How many direct competitors exist?
Are current solutions weak or generic?
Is there a clear gap in the market?
Can you differentiate clearly from existing options?
Is the space not yet saturated with specialists?
Your Expertise (1–5)
Do you have proven results in this area?
Can you speak the client's language fluently?
Do you have 3+ case studies or outcomes?
Is your methodology already developed?
Joy Factor: does this work energise you long-term?
Score each factor 1–5. Niches scoring 12+ across all three dimensions are worth testing in public for 30 days.
