Platform Selection
Choose where your client
is, not where you like.
Platform selection is an audience decision, not a personal one.

Most creators pick platforms based on what they find interesting or where the algorithm is buzzing this quarter. This is backwards.

Choose platforms where your ideal client actually spends time — not where the algorithm is exciting. If your target is B2B founders, LinkedIn + newsletter beats Instagram + TikTok every time, regardless of what's trending.

Each platform you add requires energy to maintain presence. Invest that energy where it compounds toward clients, not just reach.

Platform audit: Ask your last 5 clients where they spend most time online. Let their answers guide your distribution strategy, not your own preferences.

LinkedIn as Hub
Create your best thinking
on LinkedIn first.
Hub and spoke: create once, distribute many.

Always create your primary content on LinkedIn first, then adapt for each additional channel. Not replicate — adapt. The thinking, insight, and core idea originate on LinkedIn. The format changes per platform.

Why LinkedIn first? It has the most demanding format requirements — professional tone, depth of insight, structured thinking. Content that works on LinkedIn almost always adapts well elsewhere. Content created for Instagram rarely holds up to LinkedIn's audience expectations.

LinkedIn is your creative lab. Other platforms are your distribution network.

Adapt, Don't Replicate
Same idea. Different
language per platform.
Replication insults each platform. Adaptation respects it.

Each platform has a native voice. Copying and pasting the same text everywhere signals inauthenticity — audiences feel it.

LinkedIn carousel → Twitter thread: Each slide becomes a tweet. Cut the professional formatting, add native Twitter brevity.
LinkedIn post → Instagram caption: Extract the emotional core, lead with the feeling, add visual context.
Newsletter → YouTube script: Add transitions, personal asides, and visual cue moments. The thinking is the same. The performance is different.

Time budget: Adapting LinkedIn content for 2 secondary platforms adds 15-20 minutes per piece. Original creation for each would take 2+ hours. Adapt.

Distribution Tools
Automate the boring parts
of distribution.
Tools that handle logistics so you focus on thinking.

Buffer or Later: Schedule posts across platforms in one interface. Write and schedule a week's content in one session. Consistent posting without daily decision-making.

Zapier: Connect LinkedIn post triggers to automatic newsletter sections, tweet threads, or Notion saves.

Make.com: More sophisticated workflows — auto-generate Twitter threads from LinkedIn posts using AI, auto-archive all posts with engagement data, notify your VA when a post crosses engagement thresholds for repurposing priority.

When to Add a Platform
Depth on 2 beats surface
presence on 6.
The rule for sustainable multi-platform growth.

A thin presence everywhere signals busyness without impact. A deep presence in 1-2 places signals expertise and reliability.

The rule: Add a new platform only when you've genuinely mastered the previous one. Mastery means: you have a system, you post consistently, and you're generating inbound from it. Until then, adding platforms is distraction with extra steps.

Most LinkedIn creators who "can't get traction" aren't under-distributed. They're under-consistent on their primary platform. Fix depth before adding width.

Platform readiness test: Are you posting 3×/week consistently? Are you getting comments from ideal clients? If not — don't add a platform. Deepen this one.
