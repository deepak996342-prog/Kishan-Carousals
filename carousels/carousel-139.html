The MVP Course Principle
One problem. One person.
One timeframe.
3 modules of transformation beats 20 modules of everything.

The most common course-building mistake is trying to cover everything you know. A 40-module course doesn't demonstrate more value — it creates overwhelm that prevents completion. And completion is what creates testimonials. Testimonials are what drive sales.

The MVP Course Principle: solve one specific problem for one specific type of person within one defined timeframe. "Help B2B consultants book 3 new clients in 60 days using LinkedIn" is a course. "Master LinkedIn for Business Growth" is a topic. The first sells. The second doesn't. Specificity is not limiting — it's what makes your course findable, buyable, and completable.

Scope test: Can you describe who your course is for, what they'll achieve, and in how long — in one sentence? If not, the scope is too broad.

Validate Before Build
Pre-sell to 5 people
before building anything.
If 5 people pay → build. If not → find out why first.

Building a course takes 50–200 hours. The validation step takes 2 hours. The math makes this an obvious decision — yet most creators skip it entirely and build for months before discovering no one wants to buy.

The pre-sell process: Write a clear description of your course (outcome, modules, price, delivery format). DM 20 people in your audience who fit the ideal student profile. Ask if they'd be interested at an early-bird discount for founding access. If 5 people say yes and pay a deposit — build the course. If not, the feedback from why they declined is worth more than months of wasted production time.

Validation threshold: 5 paying students before you record module 1. Their payment is the market's vote. Their reasons for declining are the product brief for the version that will sell.

Design for Completion
Design for what students
DO, not just what they know.
Outcomes over information. Action prompts in every module.

The completion rate for most online courses is under 15%. The creators who sustain testimonial-generating sales have completion rates of 60–80%. The difference isn't production quality — it's design philosophy.

Courses with high completion rates share three characteristics: Each module ends with a specific action (not just learning), the next action builds on the last (momentum compounds), and students are shown their progress clearly (recognition reinforces continuation).

Every module should prompt the student to do something before moving to the next. Not "understand X" — "do X." The transformation happens in the doing, not the watching. Design for the action, not the information.

Course Pricing
Price to your proof.
Increase as results accumulate.
Your price is not a statement about your value — it's a hypothesis.

Course pricing follows a clear ladder tied to format, depth, and social proof:

Workshop / mini-course (1–3 hours): £97–£297. Fast, focused, high completion. Great entry point.
Full self-paced course (5–15 hours): £297–£997. Comprehensive transformation. Requires 10+ testimonials to sustain full price.
Cohort with live support (8–12 weeks): £997–£2,997. Your time + community + accountability. Premium experience commands premium price.

Start at the lower range of each tier. Build social proof. Raise the price with each cohort or version as results accumulate. A course with 50 testimonials can charge 3× what a new course with none can.

LinkedIn Launch Strategy
30 days of content before
you open enrolment.
Your audience needs to be warm before the cart opens.

The 30-day pre-launch is the most underused lever in course marketing. Use it to educate your audience on the problem, share behind-the-scenes of building the course, collect testimonials from beta students, and build a waitlist through lead magnet content.

Launch week: Daily posts Monday through Friday. Monday: announcement + early bird offer. Tuesday: detailed breakdown of what's inside. Wednesday: student result or case study. Thursday: answer objections (FAQ post). Friday: last chance + close.

Personal DMs to warm connections on launch day convert 5–10× better than public posts alone. Every post should link to a waitlist or directly to enrolment.

Launch sequence: 30-day pre-launch content → waitlist build → launch week daily posts → personal DMs to warm audience → testimonial posts post-launch
