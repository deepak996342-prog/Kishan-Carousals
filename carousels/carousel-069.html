The Fundamental Rule
Write like a human,
not a marketer.
People read personal emails next to messages from friends.

The moment your email sounds corporate — 'We're excited to share' — defenses go up. Skim mode activates.

The best email copy reads like a message from a smart, trusted friend who happens to know exactly what you're dealing with. That tone is what makes people stop scanning and actually read.

The Opening Sentence
The opening sentence that determines
whether they read anything else.
The only job of the opening line: create a reason to read the second.

Best opening sentences: start mid-story, make a bold claim, or ask a question they're thinking.

Worst: 'I hope this email finds you well.' They delete it before the second sentence.

The rule: your first sentence must create a reason to read the second. It's the only job of the opening line. Nothing else. No pleasantries.

The One-Idea Rule
Why focused emails
get better results.
Every additional element reduces the probability of any element being acted upon.

The most acted-on emails contain one idea. The most deleted emails contain: newsletter highlights, blog post links, an event, a product, and a survey.

Every additional element reduces the probability of any element being acted upon. Choose one thing per email. Do it exceptionally. Link to everything else from that one thing.

The PS Line
The most important part
you keep forgetting.
Readers skim to the PS before deciding whether to read the body.

Research shows readers skim to the PS before deciding whether to read the body. Your PS is your second headline.

Use it: 'PS — if you're dealing with [specific problem], reply and tell me. I respond to every email.' Or: 'PS — the most-read thing I shared this week: [link].'

Never waste the PS on 'Sent from iPhone.'

Words That Kill Conversions
Words that kill email conversions
(and what to use instead).
Specificity is the antidote to marketing speak.

Kill list: 'revolutionary', 'world-class', 'game-changing', 'synergy', 'leverage'. These trigger the 'marketing speak' filter.

Replace with: specific numbers, concrete outcomes, real client names (with permission), exact before/after scenarios.

The more specific your copy, the more trustworthy you appear. Specificity is the antidote to marketing speak.

Email Copy Checklist

7 elements — review every email before sending

Element Pass / Fail Criteria
Subject Line Does it create curiosity or make a specific promise? Would YOU open it?
Opening Sentence Does it give a reason to read the second sentence? No pleasantries. No 'I hope this finds you well.'
One-Idea Rule Is there one clear focus? Remove every element that isn't that one idea.
AIDA Structure Does it move through Attention → Interest → Desire → Action in that order?
CTA Is there one clear call to action? One only. Not three options. Not 'click here or reply or share.'
PS Line Does the PS work as a standalone hook? Would someone who only reads the PS be intrigued?
Human Tone Read it aloud. Would you say this to a colleague? If not — rewrite it.
