Trigger-Based Approach
The trigger-based DM approach.
Every meaningful engagement is a trigger.

Someone replies to a Story → respond with a question. Someone saves your carousel → 'Glad that was useful — are you working on [related topic]?' Someone watches your Reel 3 times → they're interested. DM with context: 'I saw you rewatched my video on X — are you dealing with Y?' Context makes the outreach feel personal, not automated.

The Framework
The DM conversation framework.
Pitch only when they ask.

1. Open with reference (their engagement, shared interest).
2. Ask one genuine question about their situation.
3. Listen.
4. Offer value (insight, resource, relevant tip).
5. Let them ask about working together — don't rush it.
Pitch only when they ask. The entire framework: provide value until they invite the conversation.

DM Mistakes
What not to do — the DM mistakes
that lose clients.
One professional follow-up after 48 hours is acceptable.

Never: open with a pitch. Send an essay. Ask 'are you interested in working together?' before building rapport. Ghost someone after an initial reply. Follow up more than twice. One professional follow-up after 48 hours is acceptable. More is harassment. Every shortcut you take in a DM destroys the relationship you built through weeks of content.

DM Conversation Flow

5 stages from trigger to conversion

1
Trigger — Engagement Signal
Story reply, carousel save, Reel rewatch, comment with intent. This is your signal to reach out.
2
Opener — Reference + One Question
'Glad that resonated — are you dealing with [their situation]?' Context + curiosity. No pitch.
3
Listen — Understand Their Situation
Let them share. Ask one follow-up question. Your job here is to understand, not to sell.
4
Value — Insight or Resource
Offer something genuinely useful. A tip, a resource, a relevant observation. No strings attached.
5
Invitation — They Ask, You Respond
When they ask 'how do you help people with this?' — the conversion begins naturally. You didn't push. They pulled.
