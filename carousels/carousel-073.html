Blog vs Business Blog
Most blogs generate traffic.
Yours should generate clients.
Different purpose demands a different strategy.

Most blogs generate traffic. Successful business blogs generate clients. The difference: alignment. Every article should serve someone at a specific stage of considering your service.
A recipe blog inspires. A service business blog should educate, build trust, and create the psychological readiness to enquire. A reader who finds your article through Google, spends 8 minutes reading it, and feels like you understand their problem is already 60% of the way to becoming a client. That's the power of purposeful blogging.

Client-Journey Architecture
Structure your blog to guide
readers through every stage.
Every article serves a specific stage of the buying journey.

Awareness articles (60%): 'How to [solve problem]', 'Why [common mistake happens]' — for people who don't know a solution exists yet.
Consideration articles (30%): 'Best approaches to [solution]', 'Case study: how I helped [client type]' — for people evaluating options.
Decision articles (10%): 'How to choose [service provider]', 'What working with me looks like' — for people ready to hire.
Your blog should guide a reader through this journey across multiple visits, warming them with each article.

Article Structure
Writing articles that rank
on Google and convert readers.
The structure that satisfies both Google and humans.

The structure that both Google and humans love: H1 (keyword + benefit) → intro that states the problem → 3–7 H2 subheadings (each answers a sub-question) → conclusion with CTA → internal links to related articles.
Word count for competitive topics: 1,500–2,500 words. Quality over quantity — one deeply useful article outranks five generic ones. Every subheading should answer a question your client is actually asking. If a section doesn't serve the reader, remove it.

The golden rule: If you removed a paragraph and the reader wouldn't notice the gap — remove it. Tight, useful writing ranks and retains.

The Blog CTA
The CTA that converts
readers into leads.
Context-specific CTAs convert 3x better than generic forms.

Most blogs end with 'thanks for reading!' That's a wasted opportunity. End every article with a relevant, specific CTA: 'If you're dealing with [the exact problem this article addressed], I offer a free 30-minute audit to show you the 3 steps to fix it.'
Match the CTA to the article's topic. An article about LinkedIn strategy should CTA to a LinkedIn audit. An article about SEO should CTA to an SEO review. Context-specific CTAs convert 3x better than generic contact forms because the reader already trusts you after spending 8 minutes with your content.

Publishing Cadence
How many posts and
how often — the real answer.
Quality and strategy beat quantity every time.

The honest answer: 2 well-researched, keyword-optimised articles per month outperforms 8 generic posts consistently. Focus on articles targeting keywords with clear client intent.
Update older articles every 6–12 months — Google rewards freshness and it's faster than writing new content. A blog of 50 strong articles outperforms a blog of 500 weak ones.
Think of your blog as a product, not a publication. Each article is a permanent asset. One excellent article ranking on page 1 delivers more ROI than 20 articles buried on page 5.

Attract New Readers

Week 2 · Consideration
Build Preference

Intent: comparison / evaluation
Word count: 1,500–2,000 words
Weeks 3–4 · Depth
Establish Authority
