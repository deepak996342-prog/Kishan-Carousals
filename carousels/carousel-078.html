Why Video Appears
Why video appears in 55%
of Google search results.
Google acquired YouTube in 2006. The integration runs deep.

Google acquired YouTube in 2006. Since then, Google has heavily integrated YouTube results into search. Search 'how to [any complex topic]' — you'll see YouTube videos in the top 5 results, often above text articles.
For service businesses, this means: a YouTube video optimised for search can appear on both YouTube and Google search simultaneously. That's two separate ranking opportunities from one piece of content. A single well-optimised video tutorial can bring traffic for years from both platforms.

YouTube vs Google SEO
Where the rules differ
between platforms.
Optimise for both separately — the signals are different.

YouTube optimisation: title keyword in first 30 characters, description keyword in first 2 sentences, specific and relevant tags, thumbnail click-through rate matters enormously.
Google video SEO: how-to and tutorial content ranks easiest. Video schema markup helps Google understand the content. Timestamps create 'key moments' in search results — when Google shows chapter links below the video in search, click-through rate increases 25–35%.
Optimise once for both. The additional 15 minutes of optimisation doubles your ranking surfaces.

Videos That Rank on Google
Creating videos that appear
in Google search results.
Target informational intent. Answer in the first 60 seconds.

Google shows videos for queries with informational intent: 'how to', 'what is', 'step by step'. Target these phrases specifically.
Record a video that directly answers the query in the first 60 seconds — Google shows video previews starting from the most relevant moment. Create a companion blog post on your website embedding the YouTube video.
This creates two ranking opportunities from one piece of content: the YouTube video ranks in video search, and the blog post with the embedded video ranks in text search. Same effort, double the reach.

The content multiplier: One video → YouTube SEO + Google video results + blog post text SEO + social shares = 4 traffic channels from 1 recording session.

Transcripts & Captions
The SEO advantage most
video creators skip entirely.
Search engines can't watch videos — they read text.

YouTube auto-transcribes videos. But adding a manual transcript to the description and a companion blog post gives Google thousands of words of text to index from one video.
Search engines can't watch videos — they read text. A well-transcribed 10-minute video contains 1,500–2,000 words of indexable content. A video with no transcript has near-zero text for Google to read.
A well-transcribed video has 3–4x more indexable content than a video with no transcript. This single step dramatically increases how many keyword variations your video ranks for.

Measuring Video SEO
Where to find your video
SEO performance data.
Two free tools give you the complete picture.

Google Search Console: go to 'Search results' → filter by 'Video'. This shows which videos appear in Google search results, their impressions, clicks, and average position over time.
YouTube Studio → Analytics → Traffic source → YouTube search: shows keyword-level data for what people searched to find your videos.
These two free tools tell you exactly which videos are working in search and which topics to create more of. Check monthly. Double down on the topics already generating impressions.

The growth loop: Check Search Console for video impressions → create more content on those topics → rank for more related terms → impressions compound.
