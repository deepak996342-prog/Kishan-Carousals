Vanity vs. Business
Vanity metrics vs. business metrics
on YouTube.
Views and subscribers are vanity metrics for service businesses.

A channel with 500 subscribers in your exact niche is worth more than a channel with 50,000 subscribers across five unrelated topics. What actually matters for service businesses: click-through rate (are people clicking?), average view duration (are they staying?), unique viewers (real reach), and — most importantly — DMs and enquiries generated.
Add one question to every discovery call: "How did you hear about me?" When the answer is YouTube, that's your real metric. That single data point is worth more than any dashboard number YouTube can show you.

4 Growth Metrics
The 4 metrics that predict
channel growth.
These four numbers tell you everything. Everything else is noise.

1. CTR: aim for 4%+. Below 3% = thumbnail problem. Fix thumbnail before anything else.
2. Average view duration: aim for 40%+. Below 30% = hook or structure problem. Rewatch your first 60 seconds.
3. Subscribers per 1,000 views: measures topic resonance. If people watch but don't subscribe, you're attracting the wrong audience for your niche.
4. Comments per view: measures engagement depth. Deep comments signal your content is creating genuine thinking and connection — the foundation of trust.

Retention Graph
Reading the audience retention graph —
what every dip means.
Every dip is a diagnosis. Every spike is a template.

The retention graph is the most underused tool in YouTube Studio. Steep drop in the first 30 seconds = hook problem. Drop at a specific timestamp = that section isn't working. Spikes (re-watched sections) are gold — these are your most valuable moments.
Use the retention graph to rewrite scripts, not just to analyse. If viewers leave at the 2:30 mark every video, something structural is happening at 2:30. Find it. Fix it. The retention graph is feedback from hundreds or thousands of viewers — it's more honest than any comment section.

Traffic Sources
Traffic sources — understanding
how people find your videos.
Optimise for YouTube Search first. It means people are actively looking.

YouTube Search: highest intent — these viewers are actively solving a problem. Optimise for this source first.
Suggested videos: discovery-based. Your thumbnail and title compete against similar content.
External (LinkedIn, email): warm, pre-qualified audience. These viewers convert at the highest rate.
Browse features: algorithmic push — earned when your CTR and retention are strong.
The strategy: build YouTube Search traffic first, then let Browse Features grow as your metrics improve. Don't chase suggested traffic before your foundation is solid.

Monthly Review
The monthly YouTube
analytics review.
20 minutes per month. That's all the data review time you need.

The monthly analytics review in 4 steps: 1. Identify top 3 performing videos — replicate the topic, format, and hook style. 2. Identify bottom 3 — retire the topic type or rewrite the hook. 3. Check which traffic source is growing fastest — double your effort there. 4. Update the thumbnail on your lowest-CTR video — this is the highest-leverage monthly action available.
Total time: 20 minutes. Don't spend more than this — you'll find diminishing returns quickly. The goal is signal, not analysis paralysis.
