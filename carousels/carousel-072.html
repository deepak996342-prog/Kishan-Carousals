The Guessing Problem
Why optimising for what
you think they search fails.
Clients search for the outcome, the pain, or the question.

Most service businesses optimise for terms THEY think their clients search. But clients search for the outcome, the pain, or the question — not the service name. 'Content strategy consultant' has 200 searches/month. 'How to get leads from LinkedIn' has 8,000.
The difference in traffic is 40x. That's the gap between guessing and researching. Research before you write. Always. The keyword gap between what you assume and what your clients actually type is where most businesses lose their SEO before they even start.

Google Autocomplete Method
20 keywords in exactly
10 minutes — free.
Google's autocomplete is a real-time search intelligence tool.

Step 1: Open Google incognito. Type your service + space. Screenshot autocomplete.
Step 2: Type '[problem you solve]' and screenshot.
Step 3: Type 'how to [result you deliver]' and screenshot.
Step 4: Type '[your service] vs [alternative]' and screenshot.
You now have 15–25 real, high-intent keywords. Free. No tool needed. Every suggestion is drawn from millions of actual searches — this is your clients telling you exactly what they want to read about.

Keyword Intent
Not all searches are equal —
intent changes everything.
Match your content type to the searcher's intent.

Informational intent: 'how to improve LinkedIn engagement' → content, guides, education.
Commercial intent: 'best LinkedIn content strategy' → comparison, reviews, frameworks.
Transactional intent: 'hire LinkedIn content strategist' → service page, direct CTA.
Your content calendar should cover all three. Top-of-funnel informs. Middle of funnel compares. Bottom of funnel converts. Most businesses only create informational content — and wonder why readers don't become clients.

The intent ladder: Guide readers from informational → commercial → transactional with internal links. Map the journey deliberately.

Long-Tail Keywords
Where service businesses
actually win in search.
Lower competition. Higher intent. Exactly your ideal client.

Short-tail: 'content marketing' — millions of searches, impossible to rank, broad intent.
Long-tail: 'content marketing strategy for B2B SaaS startups' — hundreds of searches, entirely rankable, specific intent.
For service businesses, long-tail keywords are where the gold is. The visitor searching a specific long-tail query is far more likely to be your ideal client than someone searching a generic term. Lower competition, higher conversion rate — this is the unfair advantage small service businesses have over large brands.

Free Validation Tools
Validate your keyword ideas
without spending a penny.
Start free. Invest in tools only when SEO is generating results.

Google Keyword Planner (free in Google Ads account): shows real search volume data.
Ubersuggest free tier: 3 searches per day — enough to validate your top ideas.
AnswerThePublic: shows the questions people ask around your topic.
AlsoAsked: maps related questions for content ideation.
The process: identify 20 keywords using autocomplete → validate the top 5 with free tools → create content around the winners. Don't invest in paid SEO tools until organic traffic is measurable and growing.

Keyword Research Framework

Match keyword type to content format

Informational Keywords
Build Trust
'How to [solve problem]'
'Why [thing] happens'
'What is [concept]'
Format: blog guides, explainers
Commercial Keywords
Create Preference
'Best [service] for [type]'
'[A] vs [B] comparison'
'[Service] alternatives'
Format: comparison posts, reviews
Transactional Keywords
Convert Readers
'Hire [service] [city/niche]'
'[Service] pricing'
'Work with [service]'
Format: service pages, case studies
Research first. Write second. Every keyword decision should be based on what your clients actually search — not what sounds right to you.
