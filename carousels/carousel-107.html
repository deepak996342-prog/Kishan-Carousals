Case for Building
Why You Should Build
the Skill Yourself First
There are moments in your career when only you can write your content.

In the early stages, your personal voice IS your brand. Before you have case studies, testimonials, and track records, the only thing that differentiates you from a hundred competitors is how you think and how you communicate that thinking. A ghostwriter can't access that yet.

Writing is also a strategic thinking discipline. Forcing yourself to articulate your ideas in public sharpens your positioning, clarifies your niche, and reveals what you actually believe. Many founders discover their real brand through 6 months of consistent writing — not before it.

Build the skill until you have something worth scaling.

Case for Hiring
When It Makes Sense
to Hire Ghostwriting Help
Three conditions that justify the investment.

Your content ROI is validated. You have data showing that LinkedIn generates leads, referrals, or opportunities for your business. Now the question is scale, not whether it works.

Your time genuinely costs more. If an hour of your time is worth $300 and you're spending 5 hours a week on content, that's $1,500/week of opportunity cost. A ghostwriter at $2,000/month is now an obvious ROI decision — not a luxury.

You have the POV but struggle to articulate it. Some people think deeply but write slowly. A skilled ghostwriter who interviews you and shapes your ideas into posts is a genuine force multiplier.

The Spectrum
The Ghostwriting Spectrum —
5 Options Between DIY and Full Service
It's not binary — find the right level of leverage for your stage.

1. Full ghostwriter — they create everything, you review and publish. Highest cost, maximum time savings.

2. Editor/strategist — you write rough drafts, they sharpen and optimise. Preserves your voice, reduces time by 60%.

3. Interview-based writer — they interview you weekly for 30 minutes, extract your ideas, write the posts. The sweet spot for many founders.

4. Template system — they build your frameworks and templates once; you fill in the specifics each week.

5. Writing coach — they develop your skills over 6 months so you need less help long-term.

Vetting Writers
How to Vet a LinkedIn
Ghostwriter Before Hiring
Three checks that separate real results from good sales pitches.

Ask for client results, not just writing samples. Anyone can write a decent post. Ask for before/after analytics: what was the client's follower count when they started? What was it 3 months later? What leads did the content generate?

Run a paid test post. Before committing to a retainer, pay for one post written to a detailed brief. Review the draft for voice match, not just quality. A technically great post that doesn't sound like you is worthless.

Check their own LinkedIn profile. A LinkedIn ghostwriter who doesn't grow their own LinkedIn audience should raise a flag. Do they practice what they sell?

Making Hybrid Work
The Hybrid Model That
Gets the Best of Both Worlds
Your voice, their craft — the collaboration that actually scales.

The most effective setup most busy professionals use isn't fully outsourced or fully DIY — it's collaborative:

You supply: your ideas, your personal stories, your unique point of view, the experiences only you have had, the opinions only you hold. This is irreplaceable and cannot be faked.

They supply: structure, hook writing, editing for clarity, format optimisation, posting cadence management, analytics review.

You always review before publishing. If you'd be embarrassed to have someone read the draft knowing you wrote it yourself — it needs another revision. You are still accountable for everything published under your name.
