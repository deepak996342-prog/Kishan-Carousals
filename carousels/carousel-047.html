Quarterly Planning
The quarterly YouTube
planning method.
Plan 3 months at a time. Leave 20% of slots open for trending topics.

Planning 12 videos at a time sounds overwhelming — until you realise it takes 90 minutes once per quarter instead of 30 minutes of stress every single week. Group videos by monthly theme or series. Monthly theme = easier to batch-create. Leave 20% of slots flexible for trending topics and timely content.
The planning session removes the single biggest barrier to consistent YouTube: the "what do I post?" decision. When that decision is made in advance, your energy on production day goes into quality — not topic anxiety.

3 Video Types
The 3 types of videos every
YouTube channel needs.
Balance: 50% SEO, 30% authority, 20% conversion.

1. SEO/Search videos (evergreen, discovery): these rank in search and bring in new viewers for years. They answer specific questions. They're the traffic engine.
2. Authority videos (showcase your expertise): these demonstrate depth of knowledge. They're why subscribers stay. They build the trust that search videos can't.
3. Conversion videos (case studies, testimonials, offers): these are where clients recognise themselves and reach out. 20% of your content can drive 80% of your enquiries when positioned correctly.

Batching Production
Batching production — creating
4 videos in one day.
Batching eliminates 80% of production friction and setup time.

The logistics: same setup, same lighting, change outfits between sessions. Script all 4 videos before recording day. Record in order of energy — most important content first, when you're freshest. Edit in sequence during the rest of the week.
The math: 4 videos batch-recorded in one day = 4 weeks of content. That's one production day per month. For most service business owners, this is the difference between "I'll get to YouTube eventually" and "my YouTube channel runs consistently while I serve clients."

Publishing Frequency
Publishing frequency — what
actually moves the algorithm.
One high-quality video per week beats three mediocre ones.

1 high-quality video per week, published consistently for 90 days, will outperform 3 mediocre videos per week every time. The algorithm rewards channels it can rely on — both for quality and schedule.
The minimum viable posting schedule: 1 video per week for 90 days without exception. Don't start at 3 videos per week unless you can genuinely sustain that volume for 6 months. Starting fast and burning out damages channel authority more than starting slow and building steadily.

Managing the Calendar
Managing a content calendar without
it becoming a second job.
30 minutes on Monday. 2 hours per week total. That's enough.

The simplest system that works: a spreadsheet with five columns — publish date, video title, status (idea / scripted / recorded / edited / live), thumbnail status, and SEO notes. 30 minutes on Monday to review and update the week. Total time investment: 2 hours per week including the planning session.
This is achievable for most service business owners without hiring a team. The calendar isn't the hard part. The hard part is committing to the system when a week gets busy. Plan for the busy week in advance — always have 2 videos in the edit queue as a buffer.
