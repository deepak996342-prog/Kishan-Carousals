Why 90 Days
Why Quarterly Planning
Works Better Than Annual
90 days is the sweet spot between specificity and momentum.

Annual planning produces beautiful documents that are irrelevant by February. Monthly planning is too reactive — not enough time to build content arcs that compound.

90 days hits the sweet spot. Short enough that you can be specific about goals, themes, and outcomes. Long enough to see compounding results. Aligned with natural business cycles — most sales, service, and product rhythms operate on quarterly timelines.

A 90-day content plan also creates accountability without rigidity. You set the direction for the quarter and make weekly tactical decisions within that framework — rather than reinventing the strategy every Monday morning.

In practice: companies that align content to quarterly goals generate 4× more qualified conversations than those creating content without strategic direction. The difference isn't output volume — it's intentionality.

Step 1: Set Goals
Step 1: Set Goals
Tied to Outcomes
Content goals that aren't connected to business outcomes are vanity metrics.

Most content plans start with "post X times per week." That's a tactic, not a goal. The quarterly content plan starts with business outcomes and works backward to content.

Business goals that content can drive:

Inbound inquiries → How many qualified conversations do you want this quarter?
Partnership opportunities → Are there 3 specific collaborators you want to reach?
Speaking invitations → Content demonstrating a POV that gets you booked?
Audience growth → A target number means a growth strategy, not just a posting frequency?

Tie every content goal to a business outcome. "Reach 5,000 followers" is not a business outcome. "Generate 10 qualified discovery calls from LinkedIn" is. The first goal produces vanity. The second produces revenue.

Goal-setting prompt: "If my content is working this quarter, what will be happening in my business that isn't happening now?" That answer is your real goal.

Step 2: Define Pillars
Step 2: Define Your
4–5 Content Pillars
Pillars are the recurring themes that make your feed coherent rather than random.

Content pillars are the 4–5 recurring themes that make up your entire content output. Every post belongs to one of them. This creates a feed that feels intentional rather than scattered.

Expertise. Demonstrates your depth in your field. Frameworks, methodologies, lessons learned. This is what you know.

Process. Shows how you work. Behind the scenes, client stories, decision-making. This is how you do it.

Results. Social proof and outcomes. Client wins, case studies, before/after. This is what you've produced.

Personal. The human behind the professional. Values, origin story, failures, lessons. This is who you are.

Hot Takes. Contrarian perspectives on your field. Disagreements, predictions, reframings. This is what you believe that others don't.

Step 3: Map to Sales
Step 3: Map Content
to Your Sales Cycle
60/30/10 is the allocation that balances trust-building with business development.

Not all content serves the same purpose in your business development cycle. A quarterly content plan allocates posts intentionally across three categories:

60% Authority-building. Demonstrates expertise and perspective. Builds the credibility that makes prospects confident before they ever contact you. Expertise, process, and hot takes posts fall here.

30% Trust-building. Creates human connection and relatability. Reduces the risk a prospect feels in reaching out. Personal stories, behind-the-scenes, origin content, and client stories fall here.

10% Promotional. Direct references to your service, offer, or availability. Asking for a conversation, announcing an opening, referencing your work explicitly. This is the content people feel most anxious about — but 10% is a remarkably small number. One promotional post per 10 is not aggressive. It's responsible.

Step 4: Build Calendar
Step 4: Create the
Monthly Content Arc
Each month of the quarter has a different strategic focus and emotional destination.

The quarterly plan distributes content strategically across three months, each with a different primary focus:

Month 1: Establish Authority. Lead with your best expertise content. Introduce or reintroduce your frameworks. Demonstrate depth. The goal is to ensure that by the end of month one, your ideal client knows who you are and what you stand for.

Month 2: Deepen With Results. Shift toward proof. Case studies, client outcomes, documented experiments, transformation stories. Authority tells people you know what you're doing. Results show them you've done it.

Month 3: Activate for Inquiry. Gradually increase the warmth and directness of your content. More personal stories. More explicit CTAs. More direct reference to the work you do for clients. By month three, your audience has seen 8 weeks of value — they're ready to hear about your offer without feeling sold to.

Planning Template

Quarterly Content Planning Template — Goals / Pillars / Arc / Cadence / KPIs

Goals + Pillars (30 min)
Goal 1: Qualified conversations (target number)
Goal 2: Partnership or speaking opportunities
Goal 3: Audience growth milestone
Pillar split: 30% Expertise · 25% Process · 20% Results · 15% Personal · 10% Hot Takes
Content mix: 60% authority · 30% trust · 10% promo
Monthly Arc (45 min)
Month 1: Establish authority — frameworks, POV posts
Month 2: Deepen with results — case studies, proof
Month 3: Activate inquiry — warm CTAs, direct offers
Weekly cadence: 3–5 posts per week, consistent day/time
Batch: Monthly 4-hr session + reactive 20% slots
KPIs to Track (45 min)
Qualified DMs received per month
Discovery calls booked from content
Profile views per week (traffic signal)
Engagement rate on authority posts
Follower growth rate (benchmark: 5–10%/month)
Block 2 hours at the start of each quarter. This session determines the commercial impact of the next 90 days of your content. Protect it.
