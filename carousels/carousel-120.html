The 5 Dimensions
The 5 Dimensions of
a Strong Personal Brand
Every brand problem traces back to one of these five areas.

Most creators focus entirely on content output — more posts, better hooks, higher frequency. But before you optimise volume, audit the five dimensions that determine whether your brand can convert.

Clarity — does your message land with strangers in 5 seconds? Is your niche specific enough to be memorable?

Visibility — are you reaching enough people consistently? Is your posting frequency sustainable?

Credibility — does proof of your work exist publicly? Are there testimonials, case studies, results?

Consistency — do all touchpoints tell the same story? Same photo, bio, tone, and frequency everywhere?

Conversion — does your reach translate into conversations, calls, and revenue? Where does the funnel break?

Clarity Audit
Running Your
Clarity Audit
The 5-second test is the most honest feedback your brand can receive.

Clarity is the first thing to audit because it affects everything downstream. A clear message converts at every stage. An unclear message fails everywhere.

The 5-second test: Ask a stranger — ideally a potential client, not a peer — to look at your LinkedIn profile for 5 seconds. Then close it. Ask them: what does this person do? Who do they help? Why should I work with them?

If they can't answer clearly, your clarity problem is bigger than your content problem. No amount of posting volume fixes a message that doesn't land.

Questions to ask yourself: Is your niche specific enough that your ideal client immediately recognises themselves? Is your LinkedIn headline a job title or a value proposition? Does your About section lead with what you do for clients or with your career history?

Visibility & Credibility
Auditing Visibility
and Credibility
Numbers and proof — the two pillars of a brand that can be found and trusted.

Visibility metrics to check: follower count and growth rate (are you gaining ground or stalling?), post reach (are impressions growing month-over-month?), engagement rate versus industry benchmark (2–4% for LinkedIn is healthy), profile view count (is your content driving people to your profile?).

Credibility assets to audit: Do you have at least 5 LinkedIn recommendations with specific, outcome-based language? Do you have 3+ published case studies or client results? Have you been featured in any media? Do you have a portfolio, a published framework, or documented experiments?

Most credibility problems are visibility problems in disguise. The proof exists — it just isn't public. Testimonials in your inbox don't count. Published ones do.

Quick credibility win: Message 3 former clients today. Ask for a specific testimonial referencing a concrete outcome. Publish all three within the week.

Consistency Audit
Auditing Brand
Consistency
Inconsistency is invisible to you. Glaringly obvious to everyone else.

Consistency is the dimension most creators neglect because they experience each platform separately. Their audience experiences all of them simultaneously — and inconsistency creates cognitive friction that undermines trust.

Check these four areas:

Same photo across all platforms. LinkedIn, Twitter/X, website, email signature — same image. Inconsistency here creates the impression of different people.

Same core bio messaging. The description of what you do should be recognisably similar everywhere, even if the length differs.

Same posting frequency. Irregular cadences — daily for two weeks, silent for three — signal unreliability. Consistent frequency, even if lower, builds more trust.

Same visual style. Colour palette, typography choices, image treatment — all consistent.

Conversion Audit
Auditing Your
Conversion Funnel
Traffic without conversion is vanity. The leak is always somewhere specific.

The final and most commercially critical dimension. Beautiful brand, great content, strong visibility — but no revenue. That's a conversion problem. And conversion problems always have a specific location in the funnel.

Profile view → conversation. What percentage of profile views result in a connection request, DM, or inquiry? If this is low, your profile isn't doing its job as a sales page.

DM → call. What percentage of your DMs convert to a scheduled call? If this is low, your DM conversations may be unfocused or lack a clear next step.

Call → client. What percentage of discovery calls convert to paid engagements? If this is low, there may be a positioning mismatch, a pricing concern, or a lack of follow-up process.

Find the biggest drop-off. Fix that first.
