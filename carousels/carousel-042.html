The Hook
The hook formula — why the first
30 seconds determine everything.
Pattern interrupt → bold claim → preview of what they'll learn.

Most viewers decide in 8 seconds whether to keep watching. Your hook must do three things immediately: interrupt the scroll pattern, make a bold claim that creates curiosity, and preview exactly what the viewer will learn or gain.
Skip the intro music, skip your name, skip "don't forget to subscribe." Lead with the value. The rest of your video earns its place only after the hook has earned their attention.

Hook formula: Pattern interrupt + bold claim + 'in this video you'll learn X' = the hook that keeps them watching

PAS Framework
The problem-agitate-solve framework
for video scripts.
Most videos skip agitation and wonder why viewers don't feel urgency.

P — state the problem clearly. Name it specifically. Your viewer should nod and think "that's me."
A — agitate by naming the costs of that problem. Time lost. Money wasted. Opportunities missed. Make the pain real without being manipulative.
S — introduce your solution. Not as a product pitch, but as the natural next step after understanding the problem.
Most videos skip agitation entirely. They go from problem to solution in two sentences and wonder why viewers don't feel urgency.

Middle Structure
How to structure the middle section
for maximum retention.
Chunked sections with clear transitions keep viewers to the end.

The middle of a video is where most viewers drop off — not because the content is bad, but because there is no structure signalling progress. Use numbered points ("here's point 2 of 5"). Preview what's coming next before each section. Break long explanations into visually distinct chunks.
This trains viewers to stay for the full list. If they know 5 things are coming and they've heard 3, they'll stay for 4 and 5. The number creates a commitment loop.

CTA Placement
The CTA placement that
doubles your conversion rate.
70% of the way through — when viewers are most engaged.

Most creators put their CTA at the end — after viewer numbers have already dropped by 40–60%. Place your CTA at the 70% mark when engagement is still high and trust has been built. Explain the CTA at 70%, repeat it briefly at the end. Never CTA in the first 30 seconds.
The psychology: a viewer who has watched 70% of your video already trusts you. They're warm. They're engaged. That is the moment to make your offer — not when they're just arriving, and not after they've started leaving.

Dialogue vs. Bullets
Writing dialogue vs. bullet points —
which works better.
Write how you speak. Your viewer will feel the difference.

Conversational dialogue outperforms bullet points for retention. Bullet-point scripts sound like a PowerPoint presentation. Dialogue scripts sound like a knowledgeable friend explaining something important.
The simplest method: record yourself explaining the concept naturally first — no script, just talking. Then clean up the transcript. What you said naturally will almost always be better than what you'd write formally. Your best lines come when you stop trying to sound like an expert and just explain it.

The 5-Part Script Framework

Every high-retention YouTube video follows this flow

Part 1 · 0:00–0:30
Hook
Pattern interrupt (first frame)
Bold claim or question
Preview of what they'll learn
No intro music or self-intro
Part 2 · 0:30–2:00
Problem / Agitate
Name the exact problem
Agitate the real cost
Show you understand their world
Bridge to your solution
Parts 3–5 · 2:00–End
Solution → CTA → Outro
3 numbered solution points
CTA at 70% mark
Brief CTA repeat at end
End card to next best video
A great script is not written — it's refined. Write, record, listen back, and cut everything that doesn't earn its place.
