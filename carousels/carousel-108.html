Why They Work
Why Recommendations Are
Your Best Social Proof
Third-party validation outperforms any claim you make yourself.

They appear at decision moments. When someone is considering hiring you and visits your profile, recommendations are the last thing they read before making a decision. A strong recommendation stack turns a "maybe" into a "yes."

They show up in LinkedIn search. Profile keywords within recommendations are indexed by LinkedIn search — meaning your recommendations improve your discoverability.

They say what you can't say yourself. You can't write "I tripled my client's revenue" in your bio without sounding like every other consultant. A client can say it for you — and it lands 10× more credibly.

The Request
The Perfect Recommendation
Request That Always Gets a Yes
Most requests fail because they ask too much. This format succeeds because it asks for too little.

Give context first: Remind them which project or engagement you're referring to. Don't make them guess.

Suggest 2–3 specific things to mention: "If you could mention [specific outcome] and [specific approach], that would be incredibly helpful." This removes the blank-page paralysis that kills most recommendation requests.

Provide a bullet template: Three optional bullet points they can use as inspiration. Most people will use them almost verbatim — and that's fine. You've made it easy.

Set a 2-week window: "Would you be able to write one in the next two weeks?" creates a soft deadline without pressure.

What Great Looks Like
The 4-Part Formula for
a Converting Recommendation
Not all recommendations are equal — structure determines impact.

A recommendation that converts follows this structure:

1. The Problem — "When I hired Kishan, our LinkedIn content was generating no leads despite posting 3× a week."

2. How They Solved It — "Kishan restructured our entire content approach in the first two weeks — new pillars, new formats, new hook strategy."

3. Specific Outcome — "Within 90 days we had 6 qualified discovery calls directly attributed to LinkedIn content."

4. Who Should Hire You — "If you're a B2B founder who wants content that actually converts, Kishan is the person to call."

4–5 sentences. Specific numbers. Present tense recommendation at the end.

Give First
The Give-First Strategy That
Gets You 3–4 Recommendations
Reciprocity is the most reliable way to build your recommendation count.

Instead of asking for recommendations, start by giving 5 proactive recommendations to people you've genuinely worked with and admired.

Write them unsolicited, without asking for anything in return. Be specific about outcomes and results. Use the 4-part formula.

What happens next is predictable: within 30 days, 3–4 of the 5 people will write you a recommendation in return. Reciprocity is a deeply human instinct — and it works even more reliably on LinkedIn where the action is visible to mutual connections.

This strategy requires no awkward asks. It positions you as generous. And it works every time.

Give 5, receive 3–4. Write your first proactive recommendation today — it takes 10 minutes and starts the reciprocity cycle.

Recommendations as Content
How to Turn Your Best
Recommendation Into a Post
Screenshot social proof = organic reach + credibility simultaneously.

Your best recommendation sitting quietly on your profile reaches only the people who visit it. That might be 50–100 people per week. But a post about that recommendation can reach thousands.

Here's the format: Share a screenshot of the recommendation. Add 2–3 sentences about what you're proud of in the outcome. Make it about the client's transformation, not your ego. Tag the person if they're comfortable being tagged.

This creates a post that demonstrates value without directly selling, leverages genuine social proof, and reaches your entire network — not just profile visitors. Post your best recommendation once every 2–3 months. It never gets old to new followers.

Recommendation System

Request Script / What to Include / Follow-Up Sequence

Request Script
Remind them of the specific project/engagement
Suggest 2–3 specific things to mention
Provide optional bullet template
Set a soft 2-week deadline
Thank them regardless of their answer
Use LinkedIn message, not email
What to Include
The specific problem they had before you
How you solved it (approach, not just title)
One specific measurable outcome
Who should hire you — a clear statement
4–5 sentences max — length hurts credibility
Present tense final recommendation
Follow-Up Sequence
Give 5 proactive recs first (no ask)
Follow up once if no response after 10 days
Post best recommendation as content 1×/quarter
Screenshot and save all recs to your files
Review your rec section quarterly — refresh old
Target 10+ recommendations total on profile
