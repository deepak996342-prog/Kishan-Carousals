The Problem With Follower Count
Follower count measures interest.
Not impact. Not income.
10,000 passive followers can be worth less than 500 engaged ones.

Follower count is a vanity metric not because it's meaningless — it's not — but because it measures potential, not conversion. A creator with 50,000 followers and no clients has a larger audience than a creator with 2,000 followers and a full pipeline. The second creator has built a business. The first has built an audience without a conversion mechanism.

The alternative is impact metrics: how many inbound enquiries did your content generate this month? How many led to proposals? How many converted to clients? How much revenue is directly attributable to your LinkedIn presence? These numbers tell you whether your brand is generating business value — and they are rarely correlated with follower count as strongly as creators assume.

Reframe: Stop asking "how many followers do I have?" Start asking "how many enquiries did I receive this month, and where did they come from?"

The 7 Brand ROI Metrics
Seven numbers that tell you
if your brand is working.
Each metric reveals a different layer of brand health and business impact.

1. Inbound inquiries (origin-tracked): How many, and from where.
2. Sales-qualified leads from content: Inquiries that meet your ideal client criteria.
3. Close rate on content-warmed leads: vs. your close rate on cold leads.
4. Speaking invitations or media mentions: Signals brand reach beyond your immediate audience.
5. Email list / newsletter growth: Audience you own, not platform-dependent.
6. Revenue directly attributed to LinkedIn: Track where every client originated.
7. Referrals from content followers: People who know you only through your content and refer others.

Together, these 7 metrics give you a complete picture of brand ROI across the visibility, trust, and conversion dimensions.

Setting Up Attribution
Ask every new client
three questions.
The pattern reveals which content is doing the real business work.

Attribution begins with a simple, consistent habit: after every new client engagement begins, ask three questions:

"Where did you first find me?" — identifies the source channel.
"What made you reach out at this particular time?" — identifies the trigger content or event.
"What content or posts of mine did you read before reaching out?" — identifies the trust-building content.

Log every answer in a simple spreadsheet: date, source, trigger, content consumed. After 6 months, the patterns become undeniable. Usually, 1–2 specific post types or topic series account for 70–80% of all qualified leads. This data makes your next 6 months of content decisions obvious.

Attribution tracker setup: Client name · Date · Source channel · Trigger post or event · Trust-building content noted · Revenue from engagement. Review quarterly.

Monthly Review (30 Minutes)
One 30-minute session that
sharpens your entire strategy.
7 metrics vs. previous month + quarter. Identify 1 series driving the most business.

A monthly brand ROI review does not need to be complex. Set a 30-minute calendar block on the last Friday of each month. Review your 7 metrics against the prior month and the quarter. The questions to answer:

"Which content series generated the most inbound enquiries this month?" — double it next month.
"Which posts generated the most saves and DMs?" — these are your trust-building workhorses.
"Is my email list growing?" — if not, add a lead magnet to your top posts.
"What is my close rate this month vs. last month?" — declining close rate signals a qualification or offer problem.

Thirty minutes per month of honest data review is worth more than 10 hours of content strategy theorising.

Brand Value vs. Revenue Gap
Some brand value doesn't
show in revenue. Track both.
Recognition and invitations are brand assets that feed future revenue.

Not all brand ROI is immediately monetisable. Speaking invitations, media mentions, peer recognition, academic citations, and inbound partnership enquiries are brand value that will often convert to revenue — on a longer timeframe. If you only track current revenue, you will undervalue the brand work that is building your pipeline for the next 12 months.

Track both: Revenue metrics (inbound leads, SQLs, close rate, revenue attributed) and brand equity metrics (speaking invitations, media mentions, peer shout-outs, inbound partnership enquiries, follower growth rate, engagement quality). The brand equity metrics today are the revenue pipeline metrics of next year. A brand that generates both simultaneously is compounding in the truest sense.
