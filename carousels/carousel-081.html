The Underpricing Problem
Why most service businesses
underprice themselves.
Pricing is your most powerful positioning signal.

A £500 project and a £5,000 project are perceived completely differently — before a single piece of work is seen. Underpricing attracts price-sensitive clients who are hardest to serve.

The business owners who charge the most often have the most satisfied clients, because they attract clients who value the work. Your price communicates your value before you say a word.

The signal: Low prices don't attract more clients — they attract the wrong clients. Raise your prices and watch the quality of your conversations change.

Value vs. Hourly
The shift that changes
everything.
Hourly billing punishes efficiency. Value pricing rewards expertise.

The faster you work, the less you earn on hourly rates — your expertise is actively penalised. Value-based pricing ties your fee to the outcome you deliver.

'I charge £3,000 for this engagement' positions you as an expert with a clear deliverable. 'I charge £75/hour' positions you as a commodity measured by time. One creates confidence. The other creates clock-watching.

3-Tier Pricing
The anchor pricing strategy
that shifts perception.
Offer 3 packages — small, medium, large. Most clients choose the middle.

The highest tier anchors perception of value for the middle tier. The lowest tier provides an entry point that often upgrades.

Without 3 options, clients compare you to competitors. With 3 options, they compare your tiers to each other — and you win either way. The premium tier does not need to sell often. Its job is to make the core tier look excellent value.

The psychology: The middle option captures 60–70% of buyers. Price it where you want to live. Anchor the top tier 3–4× above it.

Raising Your Rates
How to raise your prices
without losing clients.
The announcement that keeps the clients you want.

Announce price increases to current clients 30 days before implementation. Give them the opportunity to book future work at current rates. Use the language: 'My rates are increasing to better reflect the value I deliver. Here's what's changing.'

Most loyal clients stay. Some leave — and that's fine. The right clients value what you do. The ones who leave were already not the right fit for where you're taking your business.

Objection Handling
The price objection response
that converts.
When a prospect says "it's too expensive" — don't discount. Ask.

Ask: 'Too expensive compared to what?' The answer reveals the real objection. Then: 'What does the problem cost you if it isn't solved?'

If the answer is more than your fee, pricing isn't the issue — communication of value is. Help them see the cost of inaction. A £3,000 fee that prevents £30,000 of ongoing losses isn't expensive. It's the cheapest thing they'll buy this year.

The rule: Never discount in response to a price objection. Discounting confirms their suspicion that your price was inflated. Reframe instead.
