The Real Objection
Why price objections
are rarely about price.
Three hidden meanings behind 'it's too expensive.'

'It's too expensive' usually means: 'I don't understand the value', or 'I don't trust the outcome', or 'This isn't a priority right now.'

Rarely: 'I genuinely cannot afford this.'

Treating it as a price objection when it's actually a value communication failure is the most common sales mistake service businesses make. Diagnose before you defend.

The diagnostic question: 'Can I ask — when you say expensive, what are you comparing it to?' The answer reveals which objection you're actually dealing with.

The Reframe Question
The reframe question that
reveals the real objection.
One question reveals what really needs to be addressed.

Ask: 'Compared to what?'

If they say 'another provider': what's different about the outcomes? If they say 'doing it myself': calculate their hours × their hourly rate. If they say 'our budget': explore whether the budget is fixed or whether ROI could justify flexibility.

One question reveals what really needs to be addressed. Don't guess which objection you're handling. Ask the one question that surfaces it.

The ROI Conversation
The ROI conversation that
makes cost obvious.
State it as a number and 'expensive' disappears.

'If this engagement brings in 2 new clients, what does that mean for your business?'

Help them calculate the return. A £3,000 fee that generates £15,000 in revenue is a 5x return. Stated as a number, 'expensive' disappears.

ROI conversations work best with commercially-minded clients who think in numbers, not just costs. Change the frame from 'cost of investment' to 'return on investment' and the entire conversation shifts.

The formula: Expected revenue from 1 new client × number of clients you typically generate. If your fee is less than the value of one result, it's not expensive — it's discounted.

I Need to Think About It
'I need to think about it' —
extracting the real blocker.
Surface the real concern and it becomes solvable.

Ask: 'Of course — what specifically would help you feel confident moving forward?'

Common answers: 'I want to see more case studies' (send relevant ones). 'I need to involve my partner' (offer a joint call). 'I'm uncertain about the timeline' (offer flexibility).

Each answer is solvable once it's surfaced. 'Think about it' is never the actual concern. It's a placeholder for something specific that hasn't been said yet.

Genuine No
Objections that genuinely mean no —
and how to handle them gracefully.
Not every no is a sales problem. Some are timing and fit.

'We've decided to handle this in-house.' 'We've signed with someone else.' 'We're pausing all spend.' These aren't sales problems — they're timing and fit issues.

Accept them gracefully: 'Completely understand. I'd love to stay in touch — might I reach out in 6 months to see how things have progressed?'

Clients who said no become clients 6-12 months later surprisingly often. The way you handle a no determines whether the door stays open.

The long game: Grace under rejection is the most underrated sales skill. Every graceful exit plants a seed for a future conversation.
