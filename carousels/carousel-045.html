The 30-Second Rule
Why 70% of viewers leave
in the first 30 seconds.
The attention decision happens before your first sentence is finished.

The average viewer makes their attention decision in 8–12 seconds. Pattern recognition happens instantly: "Is this for me?"
A slow intro, logo animation, or "hey guys welcome back" loses them immediately. These opening patterns signal to the viewer's brain: "this creator doesn't value my time." The viewer has hundreds of alternatives one swipe away. Your first 30 seconds is not an introduction — it's a contract. You're promising a specific value in exchange for their specific time. Make that contract explicit, immediately.

5 Hook Formulas
The 5 hook formulas that work
across every niche.
Test all five. Double down on whichever your audience responds to most.

1. Contrarian: "Everyone says X. Here's why that's wrong."
2. Question: "Why do [common thing] always fail?"
3. Story: "Last week a client told me something that changed how I think about [topic]."
4. Statistic: "X% of [your audience] makes this mistake — and it's costing them Y."
5. Bold promise: "In the next 8 minutes I'll show you exactly how to [specific result]."
The best hook for your channel depends on your audience. Test all five in your first 10 videos.

Pattern Interrupt
The pattern interrupt — stopping
the scroll in 3 seconds.
Your first frame matters as much as your thumbnail.

Start in the middle of action or with a bold, surprising statement. Cut the preamble entirely. No "so today I want to talk about..." No "before we get started..." These are preamble patterns that signal more preamble is coming — and viewers leave.
Your viewer's first thought when the pattern interrupt works: "Wait, what?" That brief moment of confusion or surprise is what makes them stop scrolling. It's not manipulation — it's the same technique a great book uses in its first sentence. Start in a moment of consequence.

Testing Hooks
Testing hooks — the 48-hour
rewrite strategy.
If a video underperforms in 48 hours, the hook is the first thing to fix.

If a video underperforms in its first 48 hours, re-upload with a new first 30 seconds. Keep the body of the video identical. This single tactic can 10x a video's reach.
YouTube's algorithm judges your video heavily in the first 48 hours. If it doesn't perform, the algorithm stops pushing it — often permanently. But a new upload resets that window. You get a second chance with the same content, just a better hook. Most creators never use this. It's the highest-leverage edit available after publication.

Long vs. Short Hooks
Long-form vs. short-form hooks —
different rules apply.
Long-form: 30 seconds. Short-form: 3 seconds. Different windows, different rules.

Long-form hook (8–12 min videos): you have 30 seconds. Use them to make a promise of transformation. The viewer is investing real time — they need to know it's worth it.
Short-form hook (Shorts/Reels): you have 3 seconds. Use an immediate curiosity gap or an emotional trigger. No setup. No context. Just a statement that demands the viewer stay for the next 57 seconds. The rules are completely different. A hook that works in a long video will fail in a Short — and vice versa. Write your hooks with the format in mind from the first word.
